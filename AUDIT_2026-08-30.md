## 4. The two findings that change the science

### 4A. The coefficient-map ceiling is substantially a SAMPLE-SIZE effect `[MEASURED]`

This is the most consequential thing I found, and it bears directly on the project's
flagship claim.

`l5_bottleneck.py` measured the per-mode params->coefficient R² at **one** training
size and concluded the map "carries about five modes' worth of generalisable
information" — stated as a property of the *map*, the obstruction no architecture
touches. I measured the same quantity as a function of training-material count
(3 seeds, POD refit per subset, evaluated on the same 12 held-out materials):

| | 12 mat | 24 mat | 36 mat | 48 mat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mode 1 R² | +0.234 | +0.759 | +0.898 | **+0.946** |
| mode 3 R² | +0.055 | +0.157 | +0.497 | **+0.580** |
| mode 4 R² | +0.076 | +0.363 | +0.493 | **+0.571** |
| mode 8 R² | −0.142 | −0.062 | +0.005 | **+0.079** |
| modes with R² > 0.5 | 0 | 1 | 1 | **3** |
| modes with R² > 0.2 | 1 | 4 | 7 | **8** |
| field nRMSE | 0.0929 | 0.0694 | 0.0537 | **0.0510** |

**16 of 32 modes are still climbing from 36 -> 48 materials.** Nothing has saturated.

**"The map carries ~5 modes" is not a property of the map. It is what 48 training
materials buy.** A17's replacement claim must be scoped accordingly. This is good
news: the obstruction is addressable, and it is the scientific justification for
finishing the 240-material scale-up.

*Honest limitation, and the cleaner follow-up:* refitting the POD basis on each
subset means "mode k" is a different function at each size, which is why modes 2 and
12 wobble. The clean version fixes the basis on all 48 materials and varies only the
regressor's training set — that separates "the basis needs more data" from "the
coefficient map needs more data". Run that before publishing the number.

## 5. What the literature says you must change

### 5A. The three claims that are currently scoopable or over-claimed `[LIT]`

**(i) L5 tested only the family the theory already condemns.** Lanthaler, Molinaro,
Hadorn & Mishra (ICLR 2023, arXiv:2210.01074) prove that *any* operator architecture
with a **linear reconstruction** step — DeepONet, PCA-Net, POD+regressor — has
expected squared L2 error bounded below by C/p on advection-dominated problems, and
that **FNO and shift-DeepONet escape the bound**. DeepONet and DeepOKAN are both
linear-reconstruction. Your own frozen protocol line 62 lists `DeepONet, FNO, WNO,
DeepOKAN` for L5; `run_l5.py:213` restricts `--families` to
`choices=["deeponet","deepokan"]`. **FNO and WNO were never run and the deviation is
unrecorded**, while `fno_model_adsorption.py` sits in the repository and
`validate.py` already gates it. "The operator rung is eliminated" is not defensible
until at least FNO is in the table.

**(ii) The L5 mechanism may have been published six months ago.** Heinlein & Taraz
(arXiv:2602.21910, Feb 2026) reportedly show DeepONet error becomes **branch-dominated**
once the internal dimension is large, with per-mode spectral bias `[LIT]`. That is
qualitatively your L5 bottleneck result. **Read it before writing.** Your
differentiators are real — parametric descriptor input rather than function input,
material extrapolation rather than condition extrapolation, a measured physical
cause via k_LDF unidentifiability, and the invariance of the plateau under
registration — but they must be claimed explicitly and up front.

**(iii) The two-wave warp has a large prior-art family.** Shifted POD (Reiss,
Schulze, Sesterhenn & Noack) exists *precisely* for "multiple transport phenomena";
robust multi-transport sPOD (Krah et al., arXiv:2403.04313); neural sPOD (Zorawski
et al. 2024) already learns the transports jointly with the co-moving fields — which
is exactly the obstruction you report as open. Add Taddei's registration ROM (SISC
2020, arXiv:1906.11008), Taddei & Zhang (M2AN 55:99-130, 2021), Welper, Ohlberger &
Rave's method of freezing, Sarna–Giesselmann–Benner monotone landmark matching, and
Kneip & Gasser (1992) landmark registration from functional data analysis `[LIT]`.
**Presented as an invention, this is a desk rejection.** Presented as a
physically-derived instantiation — the correct registration is dictated by isotherm
type, which nobody has claimed — it stands.

**Also: the two-wave breakthrough itself is experimentally documented prior art.**
Bozbiyik et al. (2017) measured Al-fumarate fixed-bed breakthrough and saw a single
step at low feed pressure and a stepped profile at higher; Lassitter et al. (*Chem.
Eng. Sci.* 285:119430, 2024) measured MOF-303 pellet breakthrough and attribute the
shape to the isotherm inflection, citing LeVan & Carta `[LIT]`. That is *good* — it
validates the model — but the novelty claim must sit on the coordinate change, not
the phenomenon.

**(iv) Do not headline the oracle number.** "0.0203 beats DeepONet's 0.0281" uses the
**oracle** warp. Any experienced reviewer reads that as a leaked-label comparison.
The honest headline is 0.0508 predicted vs 0.0510 fixed frame — a tie — with a
measured 2.17x of unrealised headroom. Leading with 0.0203 would destroy the one
thing this project has that its competitors do not.

### 5B. The physics the domain literature says you must fix `[LIT]`

**The k_LDF / d_p inconsistency, which also solves the Damköhler problem.** The
dataset samples `k_LDF in (0.002, 0.05)` **independently** while fixing `d_p = 2 mm`.
But k_LDF is *determined* by particle size through Glueckauf,
`k_LDF = 15 eps_p D_p / (R_p^2 rho_p dq*/dc)`. A 2 mm MOF-303 pellet at v = 0.1 m/s
gives **Da ~ 51**; a 3 mm pellet at v = 0.3 m/s gives **Da ~ 8** `[LIT]`. Your dataset
has 2 mm particles with kinetics 10–50x faster than a 2 mm particle can have.

`[MEASURED]` Your actual distribution: Da = k_LDF·t_final has p05 122, **median 626**,
p95 3312; only **0.30%** of samples have Da < 50. Reaching Da ~ 10 needs
k_LDF ~ 3.9e-4, a factor 6 below the current lower bound.

**Deriving k_LDF from d_p fixes both problems at once** — it makes the materials
physically consistent *and* lands the dataset in Da ~ 8–60, the informative,
kinetically-controlled regime where L6's H1 is actually testable. Do this before
finishing the scale-up.

**Two closure simplifications the 2024–26 literature contradicts** `[LIT]`:
- q_st is a *decreasing* function of loading in every measured AWH framework
  (typically −10 to −15 kJ/mol from zero coverage to pore filling). Your dual-term
  Do–Do gives exactly −ΔH at every loading by construction — which is what makes the
  Clausius–Clapeyron gate pass to 0.004%. Minimal fix: give the Henry and cluster
  terms different ΔH; note that gate B7 must then be restated as a bound, not an
  identity.
- Lassitter et al. (2024) find LDF is good for water in MOF-303 **except at the
  isotherm step**, where the transport rate D_s/r² has a *minimum* — a thermodynamic

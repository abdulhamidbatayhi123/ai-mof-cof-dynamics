\begin{abstract}
\noindent How fast a porous solid takes up a gas or vapour, its uptake rate law, decides how
an adsorption separator or a water harvester performs. Equation-discovery methods promise
to learn this law, not only its coefficient, from column experiments. The one published test recovered the standard law at a single rate and
noise level, with the equilibrium isotherm supplied exactly. Nobody knows in which regimes
such discovery works, or whether it fails where the rate constant stops being
identifiable from the data. We propose a pre-registered simulation study on a verified
model of a non-isothermal column. It varies how fast uptake is compared with the passage
of the adsorption front (the Damk\"ohler number, over four decades), dispersion,
noise, error in the supplied isotherm, what is measured (interior probes, or
only the outlet), the isotherm's shape, including a stepped water isotherm like that of
MOF-303, and the true law. The strongest discovery methods will be compared with the
classical identifiability of the rate constant on the same data. Three confirmatory
hypotheses rest on interior measurements and are decided by pre-declared rules whose
simulated power is at least 0.95 for effects of 0.4 decade; the outlet-only case is
reported descriptively.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection*{The question}
A fixed-bed adsorption experiment reports concentrations and temperatures in time,
usually at the column outlet. The quantity an engineer needs from it is the uptake rate
law, most often written as the linear driving force (LDF), $\partial q/\partial t =
k\,(\qs(c,T) - q)$. AI equation-discovery methods offer to find that law, not only its
coefficient. Sparse regression over a library of candidate terms
\citep{brunton2016,rudy2016}, its weak-form \citep{messenger2021ode,messenger2021pde,
reinbold2020}, ensemble \citep{fasel2022}, Bayesian \citep{hirsh2021}, implicit
\citep{kaheman2020} and exact mixed-integer \citep{bertsimas2022} variants, symbolic
regression \citep{cranmer2023,udrescu2020} and neural surrogates embedded in the
governing equations \citep{santana2023} all make that offer. This study asks a narrower
question, which can be tested:

\begin{quote}
\emph{For which regimes can the adsorption rate law be discovered from data a column
experiment can produce, and does discoverability fail at the same boundary as the
classical practical identifiability of the rate constant, or earlier?}
\end{quote}

\subsection*{What is known and what is not new}
\label{sec:notnew}
The physical core of the expected failure is classical and is not claimed here. Near
local equilibrium the mass-transfer coefficient is poorly determined by column data.
Valocchi derived local-equilibrium validity criteria from temporal moments, in which
velocity, dispersion and rate jointly decide whether local equilibrium holds
\citep{valocchi1985}. Hansen and Vesselinov showed that those criteria really test
whether mass-transfer-driven dispersion is negligible next to hydrodynamic dispersion
\citep{hansen2017}. Katsoulas et al.\ derived asymptotically that, near local
equilibrium, finite mass-transfer rates enter only through an apparent dispersion
coefficient \citep{katsoulas2023}. In adsorption engineering, Knox et al.\ found that the
LDF coefficient and the axial dispersion can be extracted wrongly from outlet
breakthrough, and that interior measurements are needed \citep{knox2016}. Ward and Pini
attributed about 70\,\% of breakthrough output variability to the isotherm parameters
\citep{ward2022}. Taylor et al.\ mapped global parameter sensitivity of sorbing transport
across P\'eclet--Damk\"ohler regimes and found that equilibrium sorption dominates
\citep{taylor2026}. The counter-evidence is also classical. In the constant-pattern
regime, a lumped rate coefficient \emph{is} extracted from frontal breakthrough
consistently with shock-layer theory \citep{miyabe2000}. ``$k$ is unidentifiable at high
Damk\"ohler number'' is therefore not true without conditions. Any boundary must be
conditioned on dispersion, isotherm shape, noise and isotherm error, and those are the
axes of this design.

The generic failure of sparse regression on such data also has precedents.
Single-trajectory identifiability of linear-in-parameter systems, the SINDy model class,
depends on the trajectory's geometry; estimation sensitivity is governed by a condition
number tied to the data's spatial confinement \citep{stanhope2014}. For both SINDy and
PySR, small eigenvalues of the moment matrix of the candidate functions over the visited
states mark combinations the data barely distinguish, and a zero eigenvalue makes distinct
equations indistinguishable \citep{gallo2026}. SINDy becomes ill-conditioned on slow
manifolds, where the recoverable object is the manifold itself \citep{delgado2025}. Sparse
identification shows noise- and sparsity-induced phase transitions \citep{klishin2024}.
When trajectories cross time-scale regimes, SINDy fails to find a single global model
\citep{muhammed2025}. Stiffness and timescale separation break rate-law discovery in
chemical kinetics \citep{prabhu2025,huang2021}. Collinear library functionals make the
discovered model non-unique \citep{delahunt2021}. One benchmark points the other way: on
attractor-filling chaotic data, SINDy performance showed no significant dependence on
scale separation \citep{kaptanoglu2023}. Breakthrough data differ from that setting. A
breakthrough run is a transient single trajectory confined near the slow manifold $q
\approx \qs(c,T)$, the setting of \citep{stanhope2014,gallo2026,delgado2025}. The design
measures this confinement directly through library conditioning (Methods,
\S\ref{sec:mech}).

\subsection*{Prior equation discovery for adsorption}
The closest prior work is Santana et al.\ \citep{santana2023}. A neural network replaced
the solid--fluid mass-transfer term of a fixed-bed model (a universal differential
equation, UDE), was trained on outlet breakthrough curves with 5\,\% Gaussian noise, and
was then read out by sparse regression (BIC-selected) and genetic-programming symbolic
regression. For the LDF ground truth the recovered form was $p_1 + p_2 q + p_3\qs$ with
$p_2 \approx -0.22$ and $p_3 \approx 0.22$. The isotherm was supplied exactly, and one
kinetic rate and one noise level were studied \citep{santana2023}. The same group used a
universal ODE on experimental multicomponent fixed-bed data \citep{nogueira2022} and
screened ten hybrid placements of neural networks in chromatography models
\citep{frandsen2025}. Differentiable finite-volume hybrids now learn constitutive laws
from transport experiments \citep{jessop2026}, and nonlocal transport kernels have been
learned from sparse breakthrough curves \citep{xu2022}. In surface science, chemical
reaction neural networks fitted to transient spectroscopy of CO on Pd(111) produced
candidate adsorption mechanisms that fit the same data equally well yet predict distinct
coverage dynamics \citep{shukla2025}: a learned mechanism, too, can be practically
non-identifiable. On the equilibrium side, neural
surrogates infer the isotherm from column dynamics \citep{praditia2022,subraveti2022}.
Bootstrap confidence intervals then reveal distinct learned sorption closures with similar
predictions, which is practical non-identifiability of a learned closure
\citep{scheurer2026}. Symbolic regression has rediscovered isotherms
\citep{haghpanah2023,sharlin2024,cornelio2021,fox2023}. Kinetic rate-law discovery by
symbolic regression with information criteria and design of experiments exists for batch
catalysis \citep{servia2024,servia2025}. Function-level identifiability of UDEs has been
separated from parametric identifiability \citep{loman2025}, and statistical tests have
been used to decide whether isotherm parameters in liquid chromatography must be
state-dependent \citep{katsoulas2026}. Three database searches made while compiling our
literature review (two OpenAlex queries and one arXiv query), re-run on 12 October 2026
together with seven further queries, found no study that discovers an adsorption rate law
from column data other than \citep{santana2023}. This supports, but does not prove, that
the regime question is open.
% Source: research/lit_equation_discovery.md, "Negative-search evidence" 1 and 2, synthesis (a);
% re-run: lit_recheck_p2.py -> audit_2026-10-12/lit_recheck_p2.json (10 queries, every hit listed).

For metal--organic framework (MOF) water sorption the gap is sharper. Water uptake on
MOF-303 has been fitted with an LDF model by hand, and the specific diffusion rate falls
markedly where the isotherm inflects \citep{abuelmaaty2024}. For flexible MOFs, the
kinetics of adsorption-induced structural transitions were resolved by time-resolved
diffraction and a hand-built model, and found to depend strongly on the framework
\citep{sakanaka2023}. A loading-dependent $k(q)$ at an isotherm step is an untested
discovery target. A 2026 Perspective on AI for MOF water
harvesting does not mention kinetics discovery in its abstract \citep{coyle2026}.

\subsection*{What this study adds}
Three things are new, if the hypotheses survive. The first is a quantitative
discoverability boundary for the strongest available discovery methods, with a mechanism:
the realised driving-force fraction against the combined error of the observations and of
the isotherm the method is given. The second is a like-for-like comparison of that
boundary with profile-likelihood identifiability \citep{raue2009} on the same grid, with
the same noise, isotherm error and observation model. The third is a test of whether an
errors-in-variables estimator moves the boundary. Isotherm error is an error in a
regressor, not in the target, and the study treats it so: the grid will use classical mixed
least-squares--total-least-squares fitting \citep{golub1980,golub1987} inside the exact
best-subset search. Errors-in-variables equation-discovery methods
\citep{fung2026,bortz2023} will be reported on their own published examples. A negative
result on any of these is publishable in the Registered Report format, and the design is
built to make it informative. Every null will carry its minimum detectable effect, and
every method that cannot be run in its strongest configuration will be reported as such.

\subsection*{Aims and hypotheses}
\label{sec:hyp}
The aim is a map of where the rate law can be discovered, set against where its
coefficient can be identified (Fig.~\ref{fig:design}). Table~\ref{tab:design} summarises each hypothesis with its
outcome measure, sampling plan, analysis and the interpretation of every outcome. Let
$\delta$ be the realised driving-force fraction, computed from ground truth as the
maximum over the observed $(z,t)$,
\begin{equation}
\delta = \max_{(z,t)\ \mathrm{observed}}\,\lvert \qs(c,T) - q\rvert / q_{\max}.
\end{equation}
Let $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}$ be the combined relative error of the equilibrium the
method is given (isotherm error $\varepsilon$), of the observations (noise $\sigma$) and,
for O2, of the reconstructed state:
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}} = \sqrt{\sigma^{2} + \varepsilon^{2} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{rec}}^{2}},
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rec}} = 0$ for O1. For O2 it is the RMS of $(\hat q -
q)/q_{\max}$ of the mass-balance-inverted uptake (M1b, Methods) against ground truth. It
will be computed once per cell from the noise-free ($\sigma = 0$) O2 observation, so it
measures the inversion's structural error; the noise is already counted in $\sigma$.
Then $R = \delta/\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}$, computed per cell. The rationale is that the
rate law's only handle on $k$ is the driving force $\qs - q$. Discovery needs that signal
to stand out from the errors that contaminate both its terms: noise on the measured state
and error in the isotherm the method is given. $R$ is the simplest dimensionless ratio of
the two, a heuristic and not a derived bound. Three of its limits are stated in advance:
$\delta$ is a maximum over the sampled points, so at high $\Da$ it depends on where the
samples fall on a steep front; $\sigma$ is relative to each channel's range while
$\varepsilon$ is relative to $\qs$; and the amplification of noise by differentiation is
not in $R$. H2a tests whether $R$ nonetheless collapses the boundary; its falsification
would show that it does not.

\paragraph{H2a: the boundary is a driving-force-versus-error boundary.}
The probability of successful discovery, $P(\text{success})$, collapses onto one curve
when plotted against $R$, across $\Da$, $\Pe$, isotherm shape and observation model (O1
and O2; O3 has no discovery method beyond M1's demonstration). The
50\,\% crossing lies at $R^{*}$ with a spread of \emph{less than half a decade} across
conditions.
\emph{Falsifier:} an $R^{*}$ spread of one decade or more across conditions.
\emph{Pre-declared words:} \verdict{R collapses the boundary}, or
\verdict{R does not collapse the boundary; the boundary depends additionally on
\{named axis\}}.
A spread of at least half a decade but less than one decade neither confirms nor
falsifies H2a and will be reported in the pre-declared words \verdict{inconclusive: R
narrows the boundary to a spread of \{x\} decades}.
% PREREG_P2 s.4.4 "Verdict words for the gaps between thresholds".

\paragraph{H2b: discoverability versus identifiability.}
For each slice ($\sigma$, $\varepsilon$, observation model, isotherm), we will compare
$\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{disc}}$, the 50\,\% crossing of $P(\text{success})$ for the best method,
with $\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{ident}}$, the boundary beyond which $k$ is no longer practically
identifiable given the \emph{true} law. Both are 50\,\% crossings from the same logistic
estimator (Methods, \S\ref{sec:ident}). \textbf{H2b is confirmatory on O1 only.} There
both boundaries rest on 45 estimation replicates per level, and the 0.25-decade decision
threshold applies. On O2
and O3, identifiability has 3 replicates on a subgrid, so those comparisons will be
reported descriptively, each with its own bootstrap interval, and carry no verdict word.
On O1 there are three pre-declared outcomes, reported in these words:
\begin{itemize}
\item \verdict{discovery fails before identifiability}: $\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{disc}} <
\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{ident}}$ by at least the MDE;
\item \verdict{discovery fails with identifiability}: the absolute difference is less than
the MDE;
\item \verdict{discovery outlives identifiability}: $\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{disc}} >
\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{ident}}$ by at least the MDE. The method then finds the law's form where
its coefficient is undetermined.
\end{itemize}
When either boundary has no crossing inside the tested range, no verdict word will be
issued and the range tested will be reported instead (Methods, \S\ref{sec:analysis}).

\paragraph{H2c: errors-in-variables methods move the boundary.}
The errors-in-variables (EIV) method on the grid (M5, Methods) shifts
$\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{disc}}$ upwards relative to the best non-EIV method at $\varepsilon > 0$;
the predicted size is a factor of 2. H2c holds where the upward shift is detectable, at
least 0.25 decade; the factor 2 grades the size (amendment A1). The comparison is like for like: both methods in the strong form (M5 is
strong-form only), on O1. The best non-EIV method over both forms will be reported
alongside, descriptively.
\emph{Falsifier:} a shift below the MDE at every $\varepsilon > 0$.
\emph{Pre-declared words:} \verdict{EIV moves the boundary by \{factor\}}, or
\verdict{EIV does not move the boundary detectably (MDE \{x\})}. A shift at or above the
MDE but below 0.30 decade (a factor of 2) will be reported as \verdict{EIV moves the
boundary by \{factor\}, detectably but by less than the predicted factor 2}, and a shift
down of at least the MDE as \verdict{EIV moves the boundary DOWN by \{factor\}}, a
negative result for H2c.
% PREREG_P2 s.4.4 "Verdict words for the gaps between thresholds".

\paragraph{H2d: the MOF case (exploratory, and labelled so).}
For a step (Type V) isotherm with a rate that drops at the step, as reported for MOF-303
\citep{abuelmaaty2024}, is the state dependence $k(q)$ discoverable, and in which
($\sigma$, observation) cells? No directional prediction is made, and no confirmatory claim
rests on H2d. Its success criterion is fixed in Methods (\S\ref{sec:metrics}).

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
Prospective methods are written in the future tense; what has already been done (the
ground-truth solves, the harness and its tests, the pilots) is written in the past tense.

\subsection{Ground truth}
\label{sec:truth}
Ground truth comes from the verified finite-difference reference solver of the companion
paper \citep{paper1}. It is a one-dimensional, axially dispersed, non-isothermal column
model, and its implementation is verified against closed-form solutions (the companion
paper's L0 checks). The uptake law is one of two:
\begin{align}
\text{L1:}\quad & \frac{\partial q}{\partial t} = k\,\bigl(\qs(c,T) - q\bigr),\\
\text{L2:}\quad & \frac{\partial q}{\partial t} = k(q)\,\bigl(\qs(c,T) - q\bigr),\qquad
k(q) = k_0\Bigl[1 - 0.8\,s\!\Bigl(\frac{q/q_{\max}-\theta}{0.05}\Bigr)\Bigr],
\end{align}
where $s$ is the logistic function and $\theta$ is the fractional loading at the
isotherm's step midpoint, computed from the isotherm at the feed temperature and recorded
per solve. Under L2 the rate falls to 20\,\% of $k_0$ across the step. L2 is implemented
through the solver's state-dependent-rate hook, whose default path is verified unchanged.
% PREREG_P2 s.4.1.
Non-isothermal physics as in the companion paper is primary; an isothermal control is
secondary. There are two isotherms (Table~\ref{tab:phys}). ``Langmuir'' is the companion
paper's default favourable isotherm for a generic sorbent. ``Step'' is water on a
MOF-303-like framework: a cooperative (Type V) isotherm with the cited step position and
capacity, the framework's packing, density and heat of adsorption, and a feed at 30\,\%
relative humidity and 30\,$^\circ$C. Column geometry, particle size and flow are the same in
both cases. Ground truth is stored with a manifest that records every parameter, the
stoichiometric time $t_{\mathrm{stoich}}$, the horizon, and a per-solve breakthrough check
(\S\ref{sec:pilots}); the manifest is public.

\paragraph{Sample and replicates.} The unit of analysis is one replicate: one
independent ($\sigma$, $\varepsilon$) realisation of the observation of one cell. There
will be 60 replicates per cell for M2--M6, M9 and the O1 identifiability profile (M8 is not

only to choose the best method of a slice; replicates 15--59 form the \emph{estimation
set}, on which every boundary that enters a verdict is estimated (\S\ref{sec:metrics}).
The frozen design had 20 replicates and selected and estimated on the same ones; amendment
A1 made both changes before any grid run (\S\ref{sec:amend}). M6 will run on a separate isothermal control: 9 solves, the 9 $\Da$
levels $\times$ $\Pe \times 1$ $\times$ Langmuir, with the energy balance switched off (T
held at the feed) and the same horizon rule. These solves are done; all reached
95\,\% breakthrough. M7, which passed its inclusion gate, needs a Julia search per
replicate and so will run on a subgrid, with the same 60 replicates so that it can take
part in the selection (the frozen design gave it 3): all $\Da$ $\times$ $\sigma \in
\{0.5\,\%, 2\,\%\}$ $\times$ $\varepsilon \in \{0, 2\,\%\}$ $\times$ both isotherms
$\times$ $\Pe \times 1$ $\times$ O1, the only observation model M7 can see
(Table~\ref{tab:obs}). M1 will run only as the minimal demonstration declared in
\S\ref{sec:compet}, nothing more.

\paragraph{Inclusion and exclusion.} No replicate, cell or method output will be
excluded. A method that raises an error in a cell will be recorded as a failure with the
error message, never dropped (\S\ref{sec:metrics}). Cells leave an analysis only where
the design already places them outside it: $R$ is undefined at $\sigma = \varepsilon = 0$
on O1 (H2a); O3 enters no H2a slice, because M1's single replicate yields no $R^{*}$; O2
and O3 enter H2b descriptively, without verdict words; and no method is run on an
observation model it cannot see (Table~\ref{tab:obs}). A boundary that has no crossing
inside the tested range is not imputed (\S\ref{sec:analysis}). Replicates are never
re-drawn: every observation is a deterministic function of (cell, replicate, $\sigma$,
$\varepsilon$, observation model).

\paragraph{Observation model, exactly (\texttt{p2\_observe.py}).}
All methods and the identifiability analysis will read data only through one observation
function, so every method sees identical corruption. O1 and O2 observe 20 equally spaced
interior probes, and O3 observes the outlet; every model samples 200 times uniform over
the solved horizon. Noise is i.i.d.\ Gaussian with standard deviation $\sigma$ times the
channel's range in that cell's ground truth. The measured isotherm is $\qsm(c,T) =
\qs(c,T)\,[1 + \varepsilon\, g(c/c_{\mathrm{in}})]$, with $g$ a smooth random function of
4 Fourier modes on $[0,1]$, normalised to unit RMS and drawn once per replicate. Every
draw is deterministic per (cell, replicate, $\sigma$, $\varepsilon$, observation model).
The true $\qs$ is never passed to a method.
% PREREG_P2 s.4.2 "Observation model, exactly".

\paragraph{Settings needed to reproduce the runs.} Each observation's random draws come from
a generator seeded by the SHA-256 hash of (cell, replicate, $\sigma$, $\varepsilon$,
observation model), so every replicate can be regenerated on its own. Time derivatives are
Savitzky--Golay first derivatives (window 21 samples, polynomial order 3). Sequentially
thresholded least squares keeps a standardised coefficient if it is at least 0.05 of the
largest, with a ridge of $10^{-6}$. The ensemble (M3) fits 100 models, each on 60\,\% of the
rows drawn with replacement, and keeps terms included in at least 60\,\% of them. The PySR settings of M7 are
those of its inclusion gate (\S\ref{sec:compet}), seeded by the replicate index. Every
setting is in the public code, and the versions of all packages are pinned.

\subsection{Candidate libraries}
\label{sec:libs}
The two libraries were fixed before the freeze to the implemented and
known-answer-tested harness.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Lib-B (equilibrium-informed, primary):} $\{1,\ c,\ q,\ T,\ c\,q,\ q^{2},\
c^{2},\ \qsm\}$. Including $\qsm$ is legitimate because it is the \emph{measured} isotherm,
carrying error $\varepsilon$. The earlier exploratory script that motivated this study
included the exact generating law, which was a defect. An earlier draft of the design
specified degree-3 polynomials, $\exp(-\theta/T)$ factors and $\qs q$ products; the tested
harness uses the smaller library above. A smaller library makes discovery easier, so the
boundaries to be measured are \emph{optimistic with respect to library size}.
\item \textbf{Lib-A (agnostic):} Lib-B without $\qsm$. Under L1 the true law is not
representable in Lib-A, because $\qs$ is not polynomial, so Lib-A cannot succeed by the
primary metric. It will be reported as held-out fidelity only. The agnostic route to the
law's form is M6 (SINDy-PI) on the isothermal control, which finds the implicit
Langmuir--LDF structure without any isotherm.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Methods compared, each in its strongest configuration}
\label{sec:compet}
The rule is that each method is run in its strongest configuration. A method that cannot be
so run will be reported as such and never replaced silently by a weaker one.
Implementation choices were fixed against known-answer systems before any grid cell was
touched.

\begin{description}
\item[M1, the S1 pipeline reproduced.] A UDE with a network in the uptake term, trained
through the column PDE and read out by sparse and symbolic regression
\citep{santana2023}. Its settings are at least those of \citep{santana2023}, and stronger
where those are weak. Feasibility was resolved by a pilot (\S\ref{sec:pilots}): M1 will
\emph{not be run at the declared scale}. It will run only as a labelled demonstration on a
minimal subgrid: Langmuir, $\Pe \times 1$, $\sigma = 2\,\%$, $\varepsilon = 0$, O3, the
9 $\Da$ levels, one replicate, L-BFGS, at most 50 steps. The O3 comparison rests on the
profile likelihood. This is a compute limit, not a finding about UDEs; on a GPU the same
code is expected to be feasible.

\item[M1b, mass-balance inversion (O2).] From interior $c(z,t)$ the uptake rate follows
from the gas-phase balance,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial q}{\partial t} = -\frac{\epsilon}{(1-\epsilon)\rho_p}\Bigl(
\frac{\partial c}{\partial t} + u\frac{\partial c}{\partial z} - D_L
\frac{\partial^2 c}{\partial z^2}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
with $q$ recovered by time integration from $q(z,0) = 0$. This is how an experimentalist
with interior probes obtains uptake. It needs no training, and every O1 method will then
run on its output, which makes it the strongest O2 competitor. Its coefficients come from
the solver's own definition, so the inversion cannot drift from the forward model. Time
derivatives use the same Savitzky--Golay rule as O1. Space derivatives use centred
finite differences, because the probe spacing is fixed by the observation model and no
smoothing window in $z$ may be tuned to the answer.
% PREREG_P2 s.3 M1b; p2/massbal.py.
On a fresh solve that is not a grid cell, the inversion recovered $q$ to within the 3\,\%
of $q_{\max}$ bound recorded in the pre-registration; the test measured a maximum
interior error of 2.4\,\% (\S\ref{sec:checks}).
% tests/test_p2.py::test_mass_balance_inversion_recovers_uptake_from_gas_data (comment:
% "measured 0.024"); commit d142180.

\item[M2, weak-form SINDy \citep{messenger2021ode,messenger2021pde}; M2b, its strong-form
counterpart.] The weak transform uses compactly supported test functions $\phi(t) =
((t-a)(b-t))^{8}$ and integration by parts, so the target needs no derivative of noisy
data. The test-function support will be swept over $\{31, 61, 121\}$ samples and chosen
per cell by the held-out weak-system residual on every fifth probe. The law will then be
refitted on all probes. A support is admissible only if its training system has at least
3 equations per library term. On the known-answer test, a long support left an
under-determined weak system that fitted a wrong, dense law with zero residual, which a
residual criterion would otherwise prefer. M2b is the derivative-based regression on a
Savitzky--Golay $\mathrm{d}q/\mathrm{d}t$. Both forms will run every sparse solver (STLSQ,
M3, M4).
% PREREG_P2 s.3 M2 row; p2/run_o1.py choose_support (WEAK_SUPPORTS, MIN_ROWS_PER_TERM = 3);
% p2/weak.py; p2_grid_o1.py FORMS = ("strong", "weak").

\item[M3, ensemble or Bayesian SINDy \citep{fasel2022,hirsh2021}.] The inclusion
probability of each term will be reported. The implemented configuration is bagged
sequentially thresholded least squares with an inclusion threshold of 0.6.
% PREREG_P2 s.3 Phase A; p2/methods.py ensemble(inclusion=0.6).

\item[M4, exact best-subset selection by enumeration.] This is the MIOSR objective
\citep{bertsimas2022}, solved exactly because the library is small and so needing no
commercial solver. The search is exhaustive over sparsity $\le 4$, BIC selects, and the
fit is \emph{unconstrained}. An earlier draft added sign constraints ($k > 0$, zero rate at
$q = \qs$). They were dropped before the freeze because ``zero rate at $q = \qs$''
imposes the very coefficient ratio $-1$ that the success metric tests, so M4 would
succeed partly by construction. Signs are judged by the metric, as for every method. BIC
carries a residual-variance floor at $2\times10^{-3}\times\mathrm{std}(\mathrm{d}q/
\mathrm{d}t)$, tied to the derivative estimator's accuracy at the grid's sampling (RMS
$8\times10^{-4}$ at 200 times). At zero noise, plain BIC kept terms of relative size
$10^{-4}$ by fitting the estimator's own systematic error. The floor was first set at
$10^{-3}$ from 400-sample tests, where M6 then bought a spurious $q^{2}$ term at zero noise
on 200 samples. $2\times10^{-3}$ is the smallest of $\{1,2,5\}\times10^{-3}$ that passes,
calibrated on the known-answer system only. These design facts were forced by
known-answer tests and declared before the freeze.
% PREREG_P2 s.3 Phase-A design fact (i); p2/methods.py best_subset(floor=2e-3).

\item[M5, errors-in-variables.] \emph{On the grid, M5 is EIV best-subset.} It runs M4's
exact enumeration, but fits each support by classical mixed least-squares--total-least-squares
\citep{golub1987}, with the total-least-squares step computed by singular value
decomposition \citep{golub1980}. Exact columns (the constant) are fitted by least squares.
Error-carrying columns and the target are fitted by total least squares, after each is
scaled by its \emph{declared} error standard deviation. For the observed channels this is
$\sigma$ times the channel range. For $\qsm$ it is $\varepsilon\,\qs$ plus its
$c$-sensitivity. Products get first-order propagation. The target's standard deviation
comes from the Savitzky--Golay filter's exact noise gain. Selection uses M4's floored BIC.
M5 will run on the full O1 grid, with 60 replicates, in the strong form. Two known-answer
tests check it: total least squares removes the attenuation bias that ordinary least
squares suffers, and at zero error EIV best-subset recovers L1 with M4's support.
M5 is given the true error levels $\sigma$ and $\varepsilon$, which a practitioner would have
to estimate. H2c therefore measures what errors-in-variables fitting gains when the error
variances are known: an upper bound on its benefit in practice. The other methods have no
use for the error levels and are not given them.
% PREREG_P2 s.3 M5 row and s.10; p2/eiv.py; p2_grid_extra.py m5 (fit_m5(obs, sigma, eps)).

\emph{ODR-BINDy \citep{fung2026} and WENDy-IRLS \citep{bortz2023} will be reported on their
own published examples only.} Both learn \emph{autonomous} ODE systems, in which every
state is denoised and given its own equation. The target here is one rate equation with
exogenous $c$ and $T$ inside a PDE, and the dominant error is in a regressor, the measured
isotherm. Extending either method to that problem would be new method development. This
was decided before the freeze. The ODR-BINDy Python port, on the authors' Lorenz example
(500 points, 20\,\% noise), recovered the exact 7-term support. Its relative coefficient
error was $5.0\times10^{-3}$, against about $2.2\times10^{-3}$ read from the paper's
heatmap, and it was about 26 times slower (2385\,s). Five noise seeds, run before the
freeze, settled whether that was scatter or a real gap: the mean relative coefficient error
over the seeds was 0.166 against about $2.2\times10^{-3}$ (a gap of about 74), and the
exact support was recovered in 1 of 5 seeds. The port will therefore be reported, in the
declared words, as \verdict{port, accuracy below the reference}, with that gap stated; M5
on the grid is unaffected. WENDy will be reported as \verdict{NOT RUN} if no
implementation is verified on its authors' own example.
% PREREG_P2 s.3 ODR-BINDy port paragraph; results/odr_bindy_verification.json:
% rel_frobenius_error 0.00498, reported 0.00224, runtime_s 2384.75; five seeds:
% results/odr_bindy_seeds.json (mean 0.16560, gap_ratio_mean 73.97), PREREG_P2 s.11.

\item[M6, SINDy-PI \citep{kaheman2020} for rational laws.] M6 will be run with Lib-A and
\emph{no} measured isotherm, on the isothermal control only (\S\ref{sec:axes}), because
$\exp(-\Delta H/RT)$ is not polynomial. Success is the implicit Langmuir--LDF structure
$\{\mathrm{d}q,\ c\,\mathrm{d}q,\ c,\ q,\ c\,q\}$ with correct signs. Its library is
$\{1,\ c,\ q,\ c\,q,\ q^{2},\ c^{2}\}$, which is Lib-A without $T$, because $T$ is constant
on the isothermal control. It will run at every $\sigma$ and at $\varepsilon = 0$ only,
because M6 is given no isotherm and isotherm error does not apply. M6 was verified on the
known-answer system, with every coefficient (hence $b$ and $k$) recovered within 5\,\%.

\item[M7, constrained symbolic regression with PySR \citep{cranmer2023}.] Julia is

\subsection{Success metric and secondary accuracy}
\label{sec:metrics}
\textbf{Success (primary)} means that the selected support equals the true law's support
\emph{and} every coefficient has the correct sign. For L1 in Lib-B the support must be
exactly $\{\qsm, q\}$, with coefficient ratio $-1$ within 10\,\%.
\textbf{Accuracy (secondary)} is $\lvert\hat k - k\rvert/k \le 10\,\%$, with $\hat k$ the
negative of the $q$ coefficient. $P(\text{success})$ will be computed per cell over
replicates. $\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{disc}}$ will be obtained from a logistic fit in
$\log_{10}\Da$, with a bootstrap confidence interval over replicates
(\S\ref{sec:analysis}). A method that raises an error in a cell will be recorded as a
failure with the error message, never dropped.
% p2/metric.py success_L1, k_accuracy; p2_grid_o1.py records errors as failures.

\textbf{Success for the other targets}, fixed before the freeze:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{M9:} support $\{\qsm\}$ with coefficient 1 within 5\,\%.
\item \emph{L2 (H2d, exploratory), operationally:} with $\qsm$ held fixed, the fitted law
$f$'s implied rate $\hat k(q) = -\partial f/\partial q$ is evaluated at the observed samples
and averaged in 10 equal bins of $q/q_{\max}$ over the observed range. The state dependence
is \emph{discovered} if and only if three conditions hold: the support contains $\qsm$ and
$q$; $\hat k$ decreases across the bins (Spearman $\rho < -0.8$); and $\hat k$ in the top
bin is at most 0.6 of $\hat k$ in the bottom bin. The true $k$ falls to 0.2 of its starting
value. This will be reported as a rate per cell, with no boundary hypothesis.
\item \emph{Lib-A fidelity:} the relative RMS error of the predicted
$\mathrm{d}q/\mathrm{d}t$ (strong form) on the held-out probes (every fifth), per cell.
\end{itemize}
% PREREG_P2 s.4.3 ("L2 success, operationally", "Lib-A fidelity"); p2/metric.py
% success_manifold, success_L2 (n_bins=10, rho_max=-0.8, drop_max=0.6).

\textbf{The best method and its selection.} H2a, H2b and H2c use the strongest method of
each slice. It is the method with the largest $\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{disc}}$ on the
\emph{selection} replicates (0--14); if no method has a crossing there, the method with the
highest mean success. Its boundary is then estimated on the independent \emph{estimation}
replicates (15--59), with a percentile bootstrap over those replicates. Choosing the best of
several methods and reporting its boundary from the same replicates inflates the boundary:
in simulation, by about 0.07 decade for the best of six equally good methods (pilot P8).
That is more than a quarter of the decision threshold, and it pushes towards the verdict
\verdict{discovery outlives identifiability}. Separating the two sets removes the bias.
The identifiability boundary is estimated on the same estimation replicates, so both sides
of H2b rest on the same 45. A method with too few replicates in a slice for both sets takes
no part in the selection. Every method's own $\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{disc}}$ will be reported
alongside.
% PREREG_P2 s.4.3 as amended by A1 (s.9); p2/analysis.py select_best; p2_analyze.py
% _best_and_boundary (N_SEL = 15); results/p2_operating_characteristics.json "selection".

\subsection{Identifiability criterion}
\label{sec:ident}
Identifiability will be assessed by the profile likelihood of $k$ \citep{raue2009} with
the true law known, at the same $\sigma$, $\varepsilon$ and observation model.
$k$ is \emph{practically identifiable} if and only if the 95\,\% profile interval
(likelihood-ratio threshold 3.84) lies within $[k/2, 2k]$. In O1 the profile integrates the
known-law ODE at each probe, driven by observed $c$ and $T$ (monotone cubic interpolation).
In O2 and O3 each profile point is a full column solve, scored against the interior $c$,
$T$ probes (O2) or the outlet $c(t)$, $T(t)$ (O3), on a coarse 25-point $k$ grid, with 3
replicates on the declared subgrid. Both PDE profiles are implemented and pass
known-answer tests.
% p2/identifiability.py profile_interval_probes (O2), profile_interval_outlet (O3);
% p2_grid_o23.py o2ident, o3. In both cases the profile refines adaptively between
the coarse neighbours of its interval. It makes up to 6 passes of 41 points (ODE profile)
or 21 points (PDE profile), each pass narrowing to the new neighbours. At low noise the
interval is narrower than one coarse step, and a single fixed refinement collapsed onto one
point below the truth on the known-answer test. The profile is given the \emph{measured}
isotherm, so identifiability faces the same isotherm error as discovery.
% PREREG_P2 s.3 Phase-A design fact (ii); p2/identifiability.py; p2_grid_o23.py run_o3:
% phys_obs.qstar_fn = obs["qstar_meas"] (information parity).

\textbf{$\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{ident}}$ will be estimated exactly like $\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{disc}}$.}
Per replicate, $k$ is identifiable or not under the $[k/2, 2k]$ criterion.
$P(\text{identifiable})$ will be fitted against $\log_{10}\Da$ by the same logistic
estimator (\S\ref{sec:analysis}), and $\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{ident}}$ is its 50\,\% crossing.
The two boundaries compared in H2b are therefore measured by one estimator.
% PREREG_P2 s.4.3 "Da*_ident, estimated exactly like Da*_disc".

\subsection{Mechanistic predictors}
\label{sec:mech}
For each cell we will record $\delta$ from ground truth, the condition number of the
library Gram matrix, and its smallest eigenvalue, following
\citep{gallo2026,stanhope2014}. These test whether library conditioning, set by how
closely the data hug $q = \qs$, predicts the boundary. They do not enter any verdict.

\subsection{Analysis plan}
\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{Boundary estimator.}
$\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{disc}}$, and $R^{*}$ under H2a, is the 50\,\% crossing of an
unpenalised maximum-likelihood logistic regression of the replicate-level success outcomes
on $\log_{10}\Da$ (or $\log_{10}R$). The confidence interval is a percentile bootstrap
(2000 resamples) that resamples replicates \emph{within} each $\Da$ level. The design is
fixed, and only the stochastic replicates are resampled. No boundary will be reported, and
no verdict word issued, when there is no crossing inside the tested range: all successes,
all failures, a fitted slope with the wrong sign, or a crossing off the grid. The tested
range will be reported instead. A boundary is never extrapolated off the grid.
% p2/analysis.py boundary(), n_boot default 2000.

\paragraph{Calibration, measured before the freeze.} On 100 simulated datasets with the
design's grid (9 $\Da$ levels, 20 replicates) and a known boundary, the estimator had
coverage 0.94 (nominal 0.95), bias $+0.012$ decade, and a standard deviation of the
estimate of 0.089 decade.
% PREREG_P2 s.4.4; tests/test_p2.py::test_boundary_ci_has_nominal_coverage_and_no_bias
% (docstring: "measured once at 100 datasets: coverage 0.94, bias +0.012 decade").

\paragraph{Decision threshold, smallest effect of interest and power.}
The verdicts of H2b and H2c compare a gap or shift between two estimated boundaries with a
decision threshold of 0.25 decade. The frozen pre-registration called this threshold the
MDE, derived as $2.8\times0.089$ decade, where 0.089 decade is the standard deviation of
\emph{one} boundary estimate at 20 replicates. That derivation is right for one boundary
against a fixed value, not for the difference of two estimated boundaries. Amendment A1
therefore keeps 0.25 decade as the threshold, names it so, and states power where it
belongs: as the probability that each rule returns the correct verdict. The \emph{smallest
effect of interest} is 0.40 decade, a factor of 2.5 in $\Da$. It is just under the grid's
half-decade spacing, the smallest difference that would change which tested $\Da$ level an
experimenter should aim at, and, with 45 estimation replicates, the smallest effect the
rules detect with probability of at least 0.95. The journal asks for power of at least
0.95.
% PREREG_P2 s.4.4 (frozen MDE, 0.089 decade calibration) as amended by A1 (s.9).

\paragraph{Operating characteristics of the decision rules (power).}
We simulated each rule with the frozen ingredients only (pilot P8, \S\ref{sec:pilots}):
planted logistic boundaries as in the calibration (slope 3 per decade), the 9 $\Da$ levels,
the frozen estimator and thresholds, and 1000 datasets per condition, at the amended 45
estimation replicates and, for comparison, the frozen 20. The two boundaries that H2b and
H2c compare were drawn independently, which is conservative if, as expected, estimates made
on the same replicates are positively correlated.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{H2b.} With no true gap, the rule wrongly returns a directional verdict with
probability 0.001 (0.054 at 20 replicates). With a true gap of 0.40 decade it returns the
correct verdict with probability 0.965 (0.869 at 20), and with 0.50 decade 0.998.
\item \emph{H2c.} With no true shift it never reports a detectable shift, on the refined or
the main grid. At 0.40 decade it reports a detectable shift with probability 0.997 on the
refined grid ($\varepsilon = 2\,\%$) and 0.962 on the main grid ($\varepsilon = 0.5\,\%$,
5\,\%). At the predicted factor 2 it does so with probability 0.855 on the refined grid.
\item \emph{H2a.} Each slice pools many cells, so its $R^{*}$ is precise. In a simulation of
12 slices of 144 cells, the spread verdict was correct with probability of at least 0.997
at true spreads of 0, 0.5, 1.0 and 1.5 decade, so precision does not limit H2a.
\item \emph{Selection.} Choosing the best of six equally good methods and estimating its
boundary on the same replicates inflated the boundary by 0.071 decade; selecting on 15
replicates and estimating on the other 45 left a bias of $-0.001$ decade.
\end{itemize}
These figures depend on how sharp the true boundary is. The standard deviation of one
boundary estimate at 45 replicates is 0.063 decade at slope 3, 0.091 at slope 1.5 and 0.044
at slope 6. For a shallow boundary (slope 1.5), H2b's power at 0.40 decade is 0.887 and at
0.50 decade 0.982, with a wrong directional verdict at no gap of 0.048; the verdicts will be
read with that in mind.

\paragraph{H2c refinement grid.}
A factor-2 shift is 0.30 decade, close to the MDE. H2c will therefore be tested on a refined
$\Da$ grid of \textbf{8 extra levels, log-uniform within $\pm 0.5$ decade of the best
non-EIV method's $\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{disc}}$} at O1, $\sigma = 2\,\%$, $\varepsilon = 2\,\%$,
for each isotherm. The 8 levels lie strictly inside $\pm 0.5$ decade, equally spaced in
$\log_{10}\Da$ with the two end points excluded. These levels need new ground-truth
solves, to be made after the main grid and before any EIV method is run on them. The
refinement is part of the design, not a post-hoc rescue.
% PREREG_P2 s.4.4; s.4.3 "H2c resolution".

\paragraph{How each hypothesis will be decided.}
\begin{description}
\item[H2a.] $R$ will be computed per cell. Because $\sigma$ and $\varepsilon$ enter $R$
itself, they are not slicing axes. A slice is one ($\Pe$, isotherm, observation model)
combination, pooling every $\sigma$, $\varepsilon$ and $\Da$ in it. $P(\text{success})$
of the best method (\S\ref{sec:metrics}) will be fitted against $\log_{10} R$ by the same
logistic estimator, and $R^{*}$ is each slice's 50\,\% crossing. Only O1 and O2 enter: on
O3 the sole discovery method is M1's single-replicate demonstration, which yields no
$R^{*}$. The spread is $\max - \min$ of $\log_{10} R^{*}$ over slices, with a bootstrap
interval. Slices without a crossing will be listed, never imputed. The verdict words
follow the thresholds in the Introduction. When the boundary does not collapse, the axis
that separates the $R^{*}$ values will be named.
% PREREG_P2 s.4.3 "R and its collapse (H2a)"; p2/analysis.py r_collapse.
\item[H2b.] For each O1 slice ($\sigma$, $\varepsilon$, isotherm), the gap is
$\log_{10}\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{disc}} - \log_{10}\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{ident}}$. Here
$\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{disc}}$ comes from the best method chosen on the selection set and
$\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{ident}}$ from the same logistic estimator, both on the estimation set
(\S\ref{sec:metrics}, \S\ref{sec:ident}). Gap $\le -0.25$ gives \verdict{discovery fails
before identifiability}; gap $\ge +0.25$ gives \verdict{discovery outlives
identifiability}; otherwise \verdict{discovery fails with identifiability}. \emph{The
hypothesis-level verdict} is the word held by at least two thirds of the O1 slices that
have a verdict; otherwise H2b is reported as \verdict{mixed: the position of discovery
relative to identifiability depends on the slice}, with the counts by $\sigma$,
$\varepsilon$ and isotherm. Slices without a crossing are counted and listed, never
imputed. O2 and O3 gaps will be reported descriptively, with intervals and no verdict
words.

\emph{Sensitivity (descriptive).} Discovery success requires the law's form and the
coefficient ratio, not the value of $k$, while identifiability concerns $k$ itself. The
verdict \verdict{discovery outlives identifiability} is meant to capture exactly that case:
the form is found where the coefficient is undetermined. To show that it is not an artefact
of the metric, H2b will also be computed with success additionally requiring
$\hat k \in [k/2, 2k]$, the identifiability criterion's own tolerance, and reported beside
the primary verdict. If the two differ, both are reported and the primary verdict stands.
% p2/analysis.py disc_vs_ident (mde 0.25), aggregate_h2b; p2_analyze.py H2b_overall,
% H2b_k_sensitivity (rows record k_hat, A1); PREREG_P2 s.2 H2b as amended by A1.
\item[H2c.] H2c will be decided at $\sigma = 2\,\%$ on O1. The shift is
\[
\log_{10}\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{disc}}(\text{M5}) - \log_{10}\Da^{*}_{\mathrm{disc}}(\text{best
strong-form non-EIV}),
\]
with a bootstrap interval. It will be estimated on the refined grid for $\varepsilon =
2\,\%$ and on the main grid for $\varepsilon \in \{0.5\,\%, 5\,\%\}$. The shift relative
to the best non-EIV method over both forms will be reported alongside, descriptively. Both
boundaries are estimated on the estimation set, the non-EIV method chosen on the selection
set. The hypothesis holds at a given $\varepsilon$ if the upward shift is at least the
0.25-decade decision threshold. The verdict words grade its size: at least $\log_{10}2 =
0.30$ decade gives \verdict{EIV moves the boundary by \{factor\}}, and between the threshold
and 0.30 decade the intermediate words of the Introduction. (The frozen design made the
factor 2 itself the criterion; a criterion equal to the predicted effect is met only about
half the time when the prediction is exactly right, so amendment A1 moved the criterion to
the detection threshold and kept the factor 2 as the graded words.) The hypothesis is
falsified if the shift is below the threshold at every $\varepsilon > 0$.
\item[H2d.] Exploratory. We will report, as a rate per cell, in which ($\sigma$,
observation) cells the loading-dependent rate is discovered under the L2 criterion of
\S\ref{sec:metrics}, with no directional prediction and no boundary hypothesis.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Multiple comparisons.} The verdicts are threshold rules applied to the point
estimates of the boundaries, not significance tests; the bootstrap intervals are reported
with every estimate but enter no verdict. The pre-registration specifies no family-wise
correction, and none will be applied. Multiplicity is instead controlled by the
operating characteristics of the rules: with 45 estimation replicates, a slice with no true
gap returns a wrong directional verdict with probability of about 0.001 (pilot P8), so among
the roughly 30 O1 slices of H2b a spurious verdict is unlikely, and the hypothesis-level
verdict needs two thirds of the slices to agree. Every slice-level verdict will be
reported. The best-method selection, the one choice made on the data, is made on
replicates that do not enter the estimate (\S\ref{sec:metrics}).

% disc_vs_ident and h2c_verdict decide on point estimates; aggregate_h2b (A1);
% results/p2_operating_characteristics.json h2b n45 gap 0.

\paragraph{External anchors (exploratory).}
First, S1 on the map. The Damk\"ohler number of \citep{santana2023} will be recomputed
under the definition of \S\ref{sec:axes}, and also in the residence-time form, and plotted.
Our literature review estimated its residence-time value at about 0.9, which is in the
kinetically controlled range. If S1 lands in our failure region, the contradiction will be

\subsection{Outcome-neutral quality checks and positive controls}
\label{sec:checks}
These checks are orthogonal to the hypotheses: none depends on whether discovery succeeds.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Freeze integrity.} The confirmatory grid drivers refuse to run unless the
pre-registration is unchanged since the most recent commit titled ``FREEZE PREREG\_P2''.
There is no override. A dated amendment is therefore made by committing the amended file
under a new such commit; the drivers then check against it, and the repository history
keeps every version.
% p2_grid_o1.py frozen(): latest commit whose subject contains "FREEZE PREREG_P2"; git diff
% --quiet against it.
\item \emph{Ground-truth validity.} Every solve must reach 95\,\% breakthrough within its
horizon; a solve that does not is extended, never silently truncated. All 81 grid solves
and the 9 isothermal-control solves passed (\S\ref{sec:pilots}). The H2c refinement solves
will be held to the same check.
\item \emph{Floor and ceiling.} A boundary with all successes, all failures, a slope of the
wrong sign or a crossing off the grid yields no verdict word, and the tested range is
reported instead (\S\ref{sec:analysis}).
\item \emph{Positive control on the grid itself} (amendment A1). In the easiest cell of each
isotherm, the lowest $\Da$ with no noise and no isotherm error on O1, the best method must
recover the law and $k$ must be identifiable in at least 90\,\% of the estimation
replicates. If this fails for an isotherm, no verdict words are issued for that isotherm's
slices (H2a, H2b, H2c): the failure is reported as the result, and its cause is sought
before anything else is interpreted.

\item \emph{Estimator calibration.} The boundary estimator's coverage (0.94 against a
nominal 0.95), bias ($+0.012$ decade) and standard deviation (0.089 decade) were measured
on planted boundaries before the freeze.
\item \emph{Information parity.} Every method and the identifiability analysis read data
through one observation function; the profile likelihood receives the same measured
isotherm as the discovery methods.
\item \emph{Positive controls: known-answer tests.} The harness is verified on known-answer
systems by a test suite (\texttt{tests/test\_p2.py}); none of these systems is a grid cell.
The components and their checks are:
\begin{itemize}
\item a synthetic LDF series obeying its law to $10^{-3}$ of the rate scale, and a
Savitzky--Golay derivative exact to $10^{-3}$ on a smooth series;
\item the success metric, which rejects a spurious term of size $10^{-6}$, a wrong sign and
a ratio error of 50\,\%, and accepts a 7.5\,\% ratio error;
% test_success_requires_exact_support_signs_and_ratio: q = -0.03 vs 0.02 rejected;
% -0.0215 accepted.
\item M4 exact enumeration, STLSQ and the bagged ensemble, each recovering the LDF law at
zero noise (M4 with $k$ error below 2\,\%);
\item the weak form, recovering the law at zero noise and at 2\,\% noise with $k$ error
below 5\,\%, and beating the strong form at 2\,\% noise;
\item the O1 runner in both forms with all three sparse solvers;
\item M1b inversion to within 3\,\% of $q_{\max}$ on a fresh solve, and the O2 pipeline

\item the differentiable column used for M1, matching the reference solver to

\item the O1 profile likelihood (isothermal, and non-isothermal with temperature), the O2

\item SINDy-PI recovering the implicit Langmuir--LDF structure with no isotherm supplied,

\item the boundary estimator's coverage and bias, and the verdict functions for H2a and

\item M8's known-answer test, kept as an expected failure that will break the suite if a

\subsection{Amendment A1 to the frozen design}
\label{sec:amend}
The pre-registration was frozen on 11 October 2026. On 12 October 2026, before any
discovery method had run on any grid cell, an internal review written to this journal's
Stage-1 criteria found four weaknesses in the frozen analysis plan. The pre-registration's
own rule was followed: each fix is a dated, reasoned amendment appended to it (\S9 of the
pre-registration), committed under a new freeze commit that the grid drivers check. The
frozen text is not edited. Amendment A1:
\begin{enumerate}
\item raises the replicates from 20 to 60 per cell and separates them into a selection set
(0--14) and an estimation set (15--59), because choosing the best method on the replicates
that also estimate its boundary biased that boundary upwards (\S\ref{sec:metrics});
\item declares 0.40 decade as the smallest effect of interest for H2b and H2c and shows by
simulation that the rules detect it with probability of at least 0.95 (\S\ref{sec:analysis});
\item makes H2c hold on a detectable upward shift, keeping the predicted factor 2 as graded
words, because a criterion equal to the predicted effect succeeds only half the time when
the prediction is exactly right;
\item adds a rule that turns the slice verdicts of H2b into one verdict, an on-grid
positive control, and a $k$-aware sensitivity analysis of H2b; and it runs M7 at the full
replicate count so that it can take part in the selection.
\end{enumerate}
A1 also corrects, as errata, statements of fact in the frozen text that no part of the design
depends on: the ground-truth solve times, the description of the step case, the bound on
$kL/u$, and the claim that the H2c refinement's end points coincide with main-grid levels
(they do only when the boundary falls on a grid level; the 8 levels lie strictly inside
$\pm0.5$ decade either way). No threshold value, verdict wording, axis, method or library
changed.

\subsection{Design limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Synthetic data.} The ground truth is a one-dimensional plug-flow-with-dispersion
model. Non-plug flow, which can corrupt the extraction of LDF and dispersion coefficients
\citep{knox2016}, is outside the model. O3 is the realistic case and will be reported
first; O1 is a ceiling.
\item \emph{Competitor strength.} The claim is only as strong as the weakest
strongest-configuration method. M1 will be run only as a demonstration because of compute.
On the grid, M5 is a classical errors-in-variables estimator, not a published EIV
discovery method. ODR-BINDy and WENDy are written for autonomous systems and will be
reported only on their own examples. M7 passed its inclusion gate. M8 fails its
known-answer test and is not run on the grid by its cost gate, measured on a shared
machine. Each will be reported in those terms. Lib-B is smaller than first planned, so the
boundaries are optimistic with respect to library size. Two methods recommended by our
literature review are not in the competitor set: physics-constrained weak-form symbolic
regression \citep{servia2024,servia2025} and partitioning by time-scale regime
\citep{muhammed2025}. Their absence is a limitation, not a finding.
\item \emph{Axis choice.} $\Da$ may not be the right single axis \citep{hansen2017,
miyabe2000}. That is why $\Pe$ and isotherm shape are axes and why H2a is stated in terms
of $R$.
\item \emph{Sampling.} Stiffness relative to the sampling interval breaks discovery in
chemical kinetics \citep{prabhu2025}. The sampling here is fixed by the observation model,
so at the highest $\Da$ the kinetic time is short relative to the sampling interval. This
will be reported, not varied.
\item \emph{Selection of the best method} per slice favours discovery in the sense that the
strongest method is used, which is the point. Its statistical cost, a boundary inflated by
choosing on the same data, is removed by selecting and estimating on separate replicates
(\S\ref{sec:metrics}).
\item \emph{Single machine.} The compute limits are those of one CPU. They decide which
methods run at scale, not what the methods can do.
\end{itemize}

\section{Pilot data}
\label{sec:pilots}
Pilot data are excluded from every confirmatory analysis. No discovery method has been run
on any grid cell. The pilots fall into three kinds. Generator pilots measured the simulator.
Feasibility and verification pilots ran methods only on known-answer systems or on fresh
solves that are not grid cells. One pilot (P8) simulated the decision rules alone.

\paragraph{P1, generator pilot.}
\emph{Measured:} for $k$ on a log grid, the residence-time and run Damk\"ohler numbers,
grid convergence ($N_z$ = 400 against 800), breakthrough and cost.
% results/p2_pilot_generator.json.
\emph{Changed:}
(i) The primary Damk\"ohler number is $k\,t_{\mathrm{stoich}}$, not $kL/u$.
(ii) At a horizon of $1.5\,t_{\mathrm{stoich}}$ the default column reached only
$c/c_{\mathrm{in}} \approx 0.75$ at the outlet. The cause is the thermal wave: the outlet
was still about 9\,K above feed. At $k = 0.01$\,s$^{-1}$ the 95\,\% crossing was at
$2.2\,t_{\mathrm{stoich}}$, and a $4\,t_{\mathrm{stoich}}$ horizon reached
$c/c_{\mathrm{in}} = 1.00$. The default horizon is therefore $4\,t_{\mathrm{stoich}}$,
subject to a per-solve check that the exit reaches 0.95; a solve that does not is
extended, never silently truncated.
(iii) The exit curve changed by at most 0.11\,\% between $N_z$ = 400 and 800 at every $k$
tested ($10^{-4}$--$10$\,s$^{-1}$), so $N_z = 400$ is adequate.
(iv) The run Damk\"ohler number $k\,t_{\mathrm{final}}$ spans about 1 to $10^{5}$ over that
$k$ range. The targeted $\Da$ range $10^{-1}$--$10^{3}$ therefore corresponds to $k
\approx 10^{-5}$--$10^{-1}$\,s$^{-1}$ at default physics.
(v) The residence-time Damk\"ohler number is $0.4\,\mathrm{s}\times k$, from $4\times10^{-5}$
to 4 over the pilot's $k$ range, which is why it is not the axis.
% PREREG_P2 s.6 items 1-4, 7 and s.4.2; results/p2_pilot_generator.json (exit_final
% 0.74-0.77 at 1.5 t_stoich; grid_change_rel <= 0.00112; Da_res 4e-5 ... 4).
The ground-truth solves have since been generated, at 1--414\,s each (median 4\,s). All 81
reached 95\,\% breakthrough within their horizon, some after extension to
$8\,t_{\mathrm{stoich}}$. Generating ground truth runs no discovery method and was
permitted before the freeze.
% p2_generate.log: "done: 81 cells; without 95 % breakthrough: none"; horizon 8 entries;
% per-solve times 1-414 s, median 4 s (RETRACTIONS.md B79).

\paragraph{P2, M1 timing pilot.}
\emph{Measured} on one cell at $\Da \approx 14$: the differentiable column matches the
reference solver to below $5\times10^{-3}$ on the exit curve, and $N_z = 50$ differs from
$N_z = 400$ by 0.55\,\%. On the study CPU a forward solve takes 22\,s, a gradient with
respect to $k$ 101\,s, and a gradient with respect to a $2\times32$ network 145\,s. That
allows about 50 training steps in the 2\,h budget, where a UDE needs hundreds to thousands.
\emph{Changed:} by the rule declared before the pilot, M1 will not be run at scale
(\S\ref{sec:compet}).
% results/p2_pilot_m1.json: Da 14.40, forward_s 21.5, grad_k_s 101.4, grad_nn_s 145.2,
% exit_change_50_vs_400 0.0055, epochs_in_2h 49.6.

\paragraph{P3, M1b verification.}
\emph{Measured} on a fresh solve that is not a grid cell: from dense exact $c$ ($N_z$ =
200), the inversion recovers $q$ to within 3\,\% of $q_{\max}$. The median error was 0.013
against a median driving force of 0.18, unchanged at $N_z = 800$, so the error is not
numerical diffusion. Fed the true $q$, the weak-form pipeline recovers the law exactly at
$\Da \approx 14$. Fed the inverted $q$, it does not; it selects $c^{2}$ and $T$. The
inversion error concentrates at the front, where the kinetic signal is.
\emph{Changed:} nothing in the methods or thresholds. The fact is declared because it bears
on H2a: for O2, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}$ must include the state-reconstruction error,
and $\delta$ will be reported against it. The definition of $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rec}}$ in
the Introduction implements this.
% PREREG_P2 s.6 item 5.

\paragraph{P4, sampling resolution of the O1 profile.}
\emph{Measured} on a fresh noise-free non-isothermal run: the profile's best $k$ at an
interior probe sits $+0.61\,\%$ high with linear interpolation between 200 snapshots,
$+0.47\,\%$ with monotone cubic interpolation, and $+0.17\,\%$ at 800 snapshots.
\emph{Changed:} monotone cubic interpolation was adopted. The bias is immaterial for the
$[k/2, 2k]$ criterion and will be reported with the O1/O2 results. The discovery methods
face the same sampling.
% PREREG_P2 s.6 item 6.

\paragraph{P5, known-answer tests that forced design facts.}
These are M4's BIC residual-variance floor (and its recalibration to $2\times10^{-3}$ at
the grid's 200-sample resolution), the adaptive profile-likelihood refinement, M2's
admissibility rule of at least 3 equations per library term, the documented M8 failure, and
the ODR-BINDy accuracy gap (\S\ref{sec:compet}). Each was found on a known-answer system and
declared before the freeze.

\paragraph{P6, M8 cost gate.}
\emph{Measured:} one M8 fit at the grid's size took 1144\,s, against the 300\,s limit
written in the pilot script before it ran. The machine was shared with other long jobs at
the time. \emph{Changed:} M8 will not be run on the grid (\S\ref{sec:compet}).

\section*{Data availability}

from the stored manifest by \texttt{p2\_generate.py}, and every corrupted observation is a

after in-principle acceptance.

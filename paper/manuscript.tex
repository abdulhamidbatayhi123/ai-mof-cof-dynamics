\title{Basis or map? A pre-registered decomposition of the error of reduced-order and
neural-operator surrogates of non-isothermal adsorption columns on unseen adsorbents}

\author{Abdulhamid Batayhi\corref{cor1}\fnref{orcid}}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\fntext[orcid]{ORCID: \href{https://orcid.org/0009-0007-0374-6755}{0009-0007-0374-6755}.}
\ead{abdulhamid.batayhi@std.yildiz.edu.tr}
\address{Department of Mechatronics Engineering, Faculty of Mechanical Engineering,
Y\i{}ld\i{}z Technical University, Y\i{}ld\i{}z Campus, Barbaros Bulvar\i{},
34349 Be\c{s}ikta\c{s}, Istanbul, T\"urkiye}

\begin{abstract}
Whether the error of a parametric surrogate on unseen parameter sets is set by its
reduced basis or by the map from parameters to the basis coefficients is rarely
measured. We measure it for a one-dimensional, non-isothermal
convection--dispersion--adsorption model of a packed bed, solved by finite volumes,
verified against \nLzeroNchecks{} closed-form solutions and compared, unfitted, to a
published breakthrough curve of the metal--organic framework MOF-303. Seven candidate
explanations of the error on unseen adsorbents were registered in advance with their
eliminating evidence and tested on \nNclustFolds\ held-out materials in five folds unless
stated otherwise, with a cluster bootstrap that corrects for choosing the best
configuration on the test materials. A perceptron on proper-orthogonal-decomposition
coefficients reaches \nCoarseTargetPct\,\% normalised error, and a per-mode learner's
error still falls as \(N_{\mathrm{mat}}^{-\nLCbeta}\) with training materials. Beyond the
first few modes the map, not the basis, limits accuracy: refining the basis
\(\nLfiveVtwoFloorDrop\times\) changes a per-mode map's error by
\nLfiveVtwoCoefChangePct\,\% and a DeepONet's by \nLfiveVtwoGainPct\,\%. Capacity is not
eliminated; Kolmogorov--Arnold networks lose to a same-size perceptron in every
comparison by point estimate (exploratory, \nNclustLegacy\ materials). The governing
equations' residual, as a training loss at a small weight, improves transfer by
\nLfourbMatGainPct\,\% (\nLfourbMatNmat\ materials); larger weights hurt, and residual
minimisation at inference makes predictions worse. Fitted closed forms are \nBfitRatio\
times worse than learning, yet a coarse finite-volume solve is more accurate than the
perceptron surrogates and cheaper below about \nCoarseBreakEven\ queries.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A surrogate built on a reduced basis, whether a non-intrusive reduced-order model with a
learned parameter-to-coefficient map \cite{hesthaven2018non} or a neural operator with a
linear reconstruction \cite{lanthaler2023nonlinear} such as DeepONet \cite{lu2021learning}, has two sources of error on a
parameter set it has not seen: the basis, which bounds how well any field can be
represented, and the map from parameters to the coefficients. Which of the two binds
decides where a method developer's effort pays, and it is rarely measured. We measure it
for a one-dimensional, non-isothermal convection--dispersion--adsorption model of a
packed bed whose parameters describe metal--organic framework adsorbents for
atmospheric water harvesting. The use case is screening: a designer choosing among
candidate frameworks wants thousands of breakthrough fields for materials that have not
been simulated, so the quantity that matters is not fit but \emph{transfer}, the error on
a material the model has never seen.

A surrogate cannot beat its own reference on accuracy, because the reference defines
the target, so a synthetic study can only measure accuracy per unit cost or accuracy
from less information. McGreivy and Hakim \cite{mcgreivy2024weak} found that
\emph{79\,\% (60 of 76)} of articles claiming to outperform a standard numerical method
in machine learning for fluid-related partial differential equations compare against
a weak baseline; that finding was the design brief for this study, which therefore
includes a coarse numerical solve as a baseline. Grossmann and co-workers
\cite{grossmann2024pinn} report that physics-informed networks did not outperform the
finite element method \emph{in their study}; we scope our results the same way.

\paragraph{Terms used throughout} The terms below are defined once
(Figure~\ref{fig:schematic} shows the objects involved).
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=0.1em,font=\mdseries\itshape]
\item[Rung.] One pre-registered candidate explanation of the surrogate's error,
tested by one experiment whose possible outcomes and their wording were fixed before
it ran. The seven rungs form the \emph{ladder}.
\item[Binds.] An intervention binds the error if improving it improves the error on
held-out adsorbents; a rung is \emph{eliminated} when the pre-declared test shows it
does not.
\item[Basis and map.] A field is written as a weighted sum of fixed spatial--temporal
patterns, the \emph{basis}, here a proper orthogonal decomposition (POD); the
surrogate's real job is the \emph{map} from the parameter set to the weights. The basis
floor (POD floor) is the error left when the weights are exact.
\item[Twin.] The data-only model a physics-informed arm is compared with: identical
architecture, data and budget, without the physics term.
\item[Codes A\(n\), B\(n\).] Entries of the correction ledger (Section~\ref{sec:corrections}
and Supplementary S1): A for a withdrawn claim, B for a caught defect.
\item[Edge rule.] An optimum found at the edge of a tuning range does not count until
the range is extended past it, unless the metric has demonstrably stopped changing.
\end{description}

\paragraph{What this paper is not} It is not an architecture paper. Rung L3
refutes the premise that a richer per-edge basis helps here, on our own data; rung
L5 refutes our own explanation of why the operators fail. The contribution is a
measurement protocol for parametric surrogates (the basis floor against the learned
map, cluster-robust and selection-corrected inference, and cost against a coarse
numerical solve) and its outcome on one nonlinear system, negative results included.

\paragraph{Prior art} Transfer to held-out \emph{systems} is established in operator
learning, by foundation models pretrained on many equations
\cite{herde2024poseidon,mccabe2023multiple}, and transfer to held-out \emph{materials}
for interatomic potentials of metal--organic frameworks \cite{krass2025mofsimbench}.
In adsorption-process engineering, MAPLE \cite{pai2020generalized} takes Langmuir
isotherm parameters as inputs and predicts cyclic-steady-state performance for an
arbitrary adsorbent, validated on a laboratory rig with two unseen zeolites
\cite{pai2022experimental}; learned surrogates have driven process optimisation
\cite{subraveti2019machine} and process-level screening of adsorbents, metal--organic
frameworks among them \cite{leperi2019development,burns2020prediction}; and operator
learning has reached cyclic adsorption with generalisation across initial conditions
\cite{ceccanti2026deep}. To the surrogate, a material is a point in the space of
isotherm and bed parameters (Section~\ref{sec:methods}). What is new here is the
combination: transient fields rather than steady-state indicators, inflected water
isotherms rather than Langmuir ones, and a cluster-robust, calibrated held-out-material
test that asks what binds the error rather than whether it is small. To our knowledge,
transfer to held-out materials has not been evaluated for operator learners on
transient adsorption fields (A23 records an earlier, broader version of this claim that
the works above falsified).

Heinlein and Taraz \cite{heinlein2026error} establish, for DeepONets on Korteweg--de~Vries and Burgers
equations, that the error is branch-dominated at large internal dimension, that the
learned trunk can be replaced by a classical basis, and that the branch shows mode-wise
spectral bias. We reproduce that signature independently and extend it: the inputs are
parameters describing a \emph{material}, evaluation is transfer to held-out materials
under a calibrated test, and beyond the first few modes the error barely moves with
basis size while the POD floor falls \(\nLfiveVtwoFloorDrop\times\), which locates it in
the map rather than the basis. How a neural operator relates to its own discretisation
\cite{bartolucci2023reno} is held fixed here: every arm uses the same encoding.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A surrogate-agnostic decomposition of held-out error into a basis term and a map
term in the optimal POD basis (equation~\eqref{eq:split}), applicable to any non-intrusive
reduced-order model with a linear reconstruction and calibrated, through an oracle given
the exact coefficients, as an equivalent mode count for gradient-boosting, DeepONet,
DeepOKAN \cite{abueidda2025deepokan} and Fourier-operator \cite{li2021fourier} surrogates (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}); because the basis
is the optimal one, the conclusion cannot depend on the basis any method learned.
\item A pre-registered test protocol for parametric surrogates: seven named candidate
explanations of the transfer error, each with its eliminating evidence declared before
the run, a cluster bootstrap calibrated on the design, a correction for choosing the
best configuration on the test materials, and every null reported with its minimum
detectable effect or the range of effects its interval admits.
\item The accuracy--cost crossover of the surrogates against a coarse finite-volume
solve of the same model (Section~\ref{sec:cost}).
\item A reference solver verified against \nLzeroNchecks{} closed-form solutions
(Section~\ref{sec:reference}) and compared, with no parameter fitted to the curve,
against a published MOF-303 packed bed whose two-wave shape and half-breakthrough time
it reproduces and whose first-wave timing it does not (Section~\ref{sec:anchor}). This
single shallow bed is an anchor, not a validation.
\item A test of a two-front change of coordinates of shifted-POD type: it reduces the
mode count when the fronts are known and gives no detectable gain when they are
predicted (Section~\ref{sec:warp}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{The governing model}
\label{sec:model}

The reference is a one-dimensional, non-isothermal packed bed with axial dispersion,
linear-driving-force (LDF) kinetics and an energy balance with wall loss. With \(c(z,t)\)
the gas-phase adsorbate concentration, \(q(z,t)\) the loading per unit particle mass,
\(T(z,t)\) the bed temperature, \(v\) the \emph{superficial} velocity and
\(u = v/\varepsilon_t\) the interstitial one, the equations integrated are
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial c}{\partial t} &= D_L \frac{\partial^2 c}{\partial z^2}
 - \frac{v}{\varepsilon_t}\frac{\partial c}{\partial z}
 - \frac{1-\varepsilon_t}{\varepsilon_t}\,\rho_p \frac{\partial q}{\partial t},
\label{eq:mass}\\
\frac{\partial q}{\partial t} &= k_{\mathrm{LDF}}\bigl(q^{*}(c,T) - q\bigr),
\label{eq:ldf}\\
C_{\mathrm{b}}\frac{\partial T}{\partial t} &= k_z \frac{\partial^2 T}{\partial z^2}
 - v\rho_g C_{pg}\frac{\partial T}{\partial z}
 + (1-\varepsilon_t)\rho_p(-\Delta H)\frac{\partial q}{\partial t}
 - \frac{4h_w}{D_{\mathrm{in}}}\,(T - T_w),
\label{eq:energy}
\end{align}
with bed heat capacity \(C_{\mathrm{b}} = \varepsilon_t\rho_g C_{pg} +
(1-\varepsilon_t)\rho_p C_{ps}\) and \(q^{*}\) the dual-site isotherm of
equation~\eqref{eq:isotherm}. Here \(\varepsilon_t\) is the \emph{interparticle} (bed)
void fraction and \(\rho_p\) the pellet density, pores included, so the solid term
counts each pellet once. Gas held inside the pellet pores is not carried as a
separate phase; it holds a negligible amount of water next to the adsorbed phase. The cluster-to-primary affinity ratio is fixed at
\nDcBH, and the cluster affinity is set per run so that the cooperative step sits at
the material's step humidity at the feed temperature. Feed concentration follows
from relative humidity through the ideal-gas law and a Magnus saturation pressure.

\paragraph{Boundary and initial conditions} Both inlet conditions are of flux (Danckwerts)
type: on the feed side of the inlet face there is no dispersion or conduction, so the
feed's advective flux equals the bed's advective plus dispersive (or conductive) flux
just inside it,
\begin{equation}
\frac{v}{\varepsilon_t}\,c_{\mathrm{in}} = \frac{v}{\varepsilon_t}\,c - D_L\frac{\partial c}{\partial z},
\qquad
v\rho_g C_{pg}\,T_{\mathrm{in}} = v\rho_g C_{pg}\,T - k_z\frac{\partial T}{\partial z}
\qquad (z = 0),
\label{eq:danckwerts}
\end{equation}
with zero gradient at the outlet \(z = L\). The bed starts clean, \(c = q = 0\), at
the wall temperature, which is held at the feed temperature.

\paragraph{Fixed properties} Every run uses \(L = \nDcL\)\,m, \(D_{\mathrm{in}} =
\nDcDin\)\,m, \(\rho_g = \nDcRhog\)\,kg\,m\(^{-3}\), \(C_{pg} = \nDcCp\) and \(C_{ps} =
\nDcCps\)\,J\,kg\(^{-1}\)\,K\(^{-1}\), \(k_z = \nDcKz\)\,W\,m\(^{-1}\)\,K\(^{-1}\) and
\(h_w = \nDcHw\)\,W\,m\(^{-2}\)\,K\(^{-1}\). Axial dispersion follows the packed-bed
correlation \(D_L = 0.7D_m + 0.5\,d_p v/\varepsilon_t\) with \(D_m =
\nDcDmDisp\times10^{-5}\)\,m\(^2\)\,s\(^{-1}\). In the larger dataset the kinetic
coefficient is not sampled but derived from the particle by the Glueckauf relation,
\begin{equation}
k_{\mathrm{LDF}} = \frac{15\,\varepsilon_p D_m/\tau_p}{(d_p/2)^2\,\rho_p\,K},
\qquad K = \frac{q^{*}(c_{\mathrm{in}},T_{\mathrm{in}})}{c_{\mathrm{in}}},
\label{eq:glueckauf}
\end{equation}
with pellet porosity \(\varepsilon_p = \nDcGlEpsp\), tortuosity \(\tau_p = \nDcGlTau\)
and \(D_m = \nDcGlDm\times10^{-5}\)\,m\(^2\)\,s\(^{-1}\). The generator therefore uses
two slightly different values of the molecular diffusivity, one in each correlation;
we report both rather than reconcile them after the data were generated.

\paragraph{Numerics and storage} Equations~\eqref{eq:mass}--\eqref{eq:energy} are
discretised by the method of lines on \nGridNz\ cell-centred finite volumes
(first-order upwind advection, central dispersion and conduction) and integrated with
SciPy's implicit backward-differentiation-formula (BDF) method at relative and absolute tolerances
\(10^{\nDcRtolExp}\) and \(10^{\nDcAtolExp}\) to \nDcSnapshots\ output times;
Section~\ref{sec:reference} verifies the solver. Each run is integrated to
\nDcHorizon\ stoichiometric times; if the outlet has not reached the acceptance level
the horizon is extended to \nDcHorizonExt\ stoichiometric times in turn, which
\nDcExtendedPct\,\% of the accepted runs of the larger dataset needed. A draw is
rejected before solving if its feed loads too little or too much of capacity, its
Henry constant is not finite and positive, its isothermal mass-transfer zone is not
resolved by the grid, or its stoichiometric time is implausible; \nDataNrejected\ of
the larger dataset's draws were rejected and \nDataNsamples\ accepted. Fields are
stored as \(c/c_{\mathrm{in}}\), \(q/q_{\max}\) and \(T/T_{\mathrm{in}}\) on a uniform
grid in \(z/L\) and \(t/t_{\mathrm{final}}\), at \(\nDcStoreV\times\nDcStoreV\) for the
larger dataset and \(\nDcStoreLegZ\times\nDcStoreLegT\) for the legacy one.

\paragraph{Damk\"ohler number} The Damk\"ohler number used throughout is computed per
sample as \(\mathrm{Da} = k_{\mathrm{LDF}}\,t_{\mathrm{final}}\), and rung L6 regresses
on its per-material median. Because the horizon is a multiple of the stoichiometric
time that depends on whether it was extended, Da carries that multiple as well as the
kinetic ratio \(k_{\mathrm{LDF}}t_{\mathrm{st}}\); for the \nDcExtendedPct\,\% of runs
that were extended it is larger than the kinetic ratio by the extension factor. We keep the
definition the pre-registration used and state this property rather than redefine
the axis after the result.

\section{The reference and its verification}
\label{sec:reference}

Every surrogate number in this paper is \emph{surrogate-versus-solver}, so the
solver of Section~\ref{sec:model} is verified rather than asserted. Its inlet is the
third- or flux-type condition of equation~\eqref{eq:danckwerts}. That choice matters, and it was initially made wrongly: our first verification compared the solver against Ogata and
Banks \cite{ogata1961solution}, who solve the \emph{first-} or concentration-type inlet. Against the correct
closed form (van Genuchten and Alves \cite{vangenuchten1982analytical}, who give the third-type solution), the
error falls to \nLzeroTracer{} absolute, an improvement of more than an order of
magnitude, with the residual maximum at the outlet where a finite column legitimately
differs from a semi-infinite solution. The two boundary conditions differ by
\nLzeroBCdiff\,\% of the inlet concentration at the front half-height at P\'eclet
number \(\mathrm{Pe} \approx \nLzeroTracerPe\), well outside an inlet boundary layer.
Van Genuchten and Alves also state that the concentration-type condition causes
mass-balance errors that grow with \(D/v\), which is the defect we found separately: a
Dirichlet ghost cell in the dispersion stencil left global mass closure off by about one
per cent until the inlet dispersive flux was set to zero, after which closure improved
to \(\nMassClosure\,\%\) (B8).

The solver reproduces \nLzeroNchecks{} closed-form solutions, each isolating one
term of the model (Table~\ref{tab:l0}), and its grid convergence is \(\nGridConv\,\%\) at
\(N_z = \nGridNz\) for the generic configuration and \(\nGridConvMof\,\%\) for the
MOF-303-parameterised one.

\section{The physics of the column}
\label{sec:physics}

\subsection{The isotherm must be inflected, with a finite Henry slope}

Water uptake in atmospheric-water-harvesting frameworks is small below a threshold
relative humidity (in our parameter space the primary sites hold a fraction
\(f_H = \nDcFhLo\)--\(\nDcFhHi\) of capacity there; Table~\ref{tab:params}), then
rises almost vertically as pore filling proceeds cooperatively, then saturates. That step is what makes these materials useful.
Single-site Langmuir cannot produce it: its second derivative is negative
everywhere, verified numerically as zero inflection points, and it predicts the
largest uptake gradient as \(c \to 0\), the opposite of the mechanism. A bare
Hill form produces the step but gives \(q \propto c^{n}\) at the origin, so
\(\mathrm{d}q/\mathrm{d}c \to 0\) and Henry's law is violated.

We therefore use a dual-term form \emph{in the spirit of} Do and Do \cite{dodo2000model}: a Langmuir
primary-site term carrying the Henry limit, plus a cooperative Sips term producing
the step,
\begin{equation}
q^{*}(c,T) = q_{\max}\left[
f_H\,\frac{b_H c}{1 + b_H c}
+ (1-f_H)\,\frac{(b_C c)^{n}}{1 + (b_C c)^{n}} \right],
\label{eq:isotherm}
\end{equation}
with \(b_H(T) = b_{H0}\exp(-\Delta H/RT)\) and \(b_C(T) = b_{C0}\exp(-\Delta H/RT)\).
Both affinities carry the same \(\Delta H\), so \(q_{st} = -\Delta H + RT\) holds at
every loading; it is reproduced to within \nIsoCCmaxErr\,\% at a quarter, a half and three quarters of capacity.

Do and Do superpose a Brunauer--Emmett--Teller (BET) primary term with a Sips term;
ours is a Langmuir primary term with a Sips term. We take from them the two-term
construction and the fact that \emph{only the primary term carries the Henry slope}
(their Sips term, like ours, has zero slope at the origin; we read their form through
Buttersack's transcription \cite{buttersack2019modeling}). Their cluster exponent is
fixed; ours is a sampled material parameter, the later model's treatment
\cite{dodo2009new}. We call the isotherm S-shaped, or stepped, and attach no IUPAC type
to it (B56). The lower end of the sampled
cooperativity is only weakly stepped: \nChemWeakNPct\,\% of the materials of the
larger dataset have \(n < \nChemWeakN\), where the cooperative term is close to
Langmuir.

Across all \nIsoNmat\ sampled materials \(n > 1\), so the Henry constant
\(K_H = q_{\max} f_H b_H\), the slope of equation~\eqref{eq:isotherm} at the origin, is
finite and positive everywhere; but the region where it dominates is often
inaccessible: for a median material the near-linear uptake regime ends at
\(10^{\nIsoHenryMed}\) of the feed humidity (Supplementary S2, Section S1).

\subsection{An inflected isotherm produces a two-wave breakthrough}

The consequence is that breakthrough is not a sigmoid. A fast, spreading wave on the
Henry branch arrives first and plateaus; a slow, self-sharpening cooperative shock
follows. The two features move \emph{independently} with material parameters, and
their separation at the outlet spans \nGapMin--\nGapMax\ in normalised time, a
factor of \nGapRange\ across the legacy parameter space, where it shapes the field
every surrogate must reproduce. On the larger dataset, at its stored time resolution,
the Henry wave crosses the column between the first two samples and the second front is
mostly a gradual, dispersive rise; the two-wave structure is present there but not
resolved well enough to align, and no verdict on that dataset depends on it.

This is established behaviour. LeVan and co-workers \cite{levan2019adsorption} give the
mechanism: an unfavourable dimensionless isotherm admits a simple wave because low
concentrations travel faster than high ones, a favourable isotherm admits no simple
wave and the solution is a shock, and a favourable isotherm with mass-transfer
resistance approaches a constant pattern. Bozbiyik and co-workers \cite{bozbiyik2017stepped} measured a stepped
breakthrough on aluminium fumarate, and report that it is feed-condition dependent:
a single step at low water partial pressure, a stepped profile at higher partial
pressure.

The two named discrepancies are visible: the modelled first wave arrives far too early, which the isotherm's low-humidity branch sets, and the shock is too dispersed, which the axial-dispersion closure sets on a bed about \nAnchorPelletsDeep{} pellets deep.}
\label{fig:anchor}
\end{figure}

\subsection{The solver against a real MOF-303 bed, with nothing fitted}
\label{sec:anchor}

Lassitter and co-workers \cite{lassitter2024mass} report a MOF-303 packed-bed breakthrough whose conditions
are fully specified in their supplementary information: a \nAnchorBedLenMm\,\si{\milli\metre}
bed in a \nAnchorTubeMm\,\si{\milli\metre} tube, ambient air at \nAnchorRH\,\% relative
humidity, \nAnchorTK\,\si{\kelvin}, bulk density \nAnchorRhoB\,\si{\kilo\gram\per\metre\cubed}.
The comparison was pre-registered before it was run. Inputs are the cited MOF-303
isotherm \cite{hanikel2021evolution,fathieh2018practical}, their bed, and declared bands for what the source does not give.

The measured curve shows first leak at \nAnchorTfiveExp\ minutes, a Henry-branch
plateau at \nAnchorPlateauExp{} of the inlet, and 50\,\% arrival at
\nAnchorTfiftyExp\ minutes. Our solver, with nothing tuned, gives 50\,\% arrival at
\nAnchorTfiftyModel\ minutes and a plateau at \nAnchorPlateauModel, with
a normalised root-mean-square error (nRMSE, equation~\eqref{eq:nrmse}) of \nAnchorNrmse\
against the digitised effluent points, against
\nAnchorNrmseComsol\ for the authors' own two-dimensional COMSOL model with fitted kinetics.
The two-wave breakthrough of Section~\ref{sec:physics} is what a real MOF-303 bed
does, and the cited isotherm produces it. As the next paragraph says, it does not
reproduce the first-wave timing (Figure~\ref{fig:anchor}).

Two discrepancies remain, and both can be attributed. The first wave arrives
\emph{too early} in the model (\nAnchorTfiveModel\ minutes against
\nAnchorTfiveExp\ measured); nothing in the declared band moves it, and what sets it
is the isotherm's low-relative-humidity branch, whose Henry fraction was fitted to
the step position and the capacity only. This is the first dynamic measurement that
constrains it, and it is a limitation of our MOF-303 parameterisation rather than of
the solver. The shock is \emph{too dispersed}: the axial-dispersion closure gives a
column P\'eclet number of \nAnchorPeCol\ on a bed about \nAnchorPelletsDeep{} pellets deep, where a
packed-bed correlation does not apply. That discrepancy is now measured rather than
attributed: Section~\ref{sec:limitations} shows that \nAnchorTcutPct\,\% of the
excess arrival time comes from evaluating one correlation at a limit this bed does
not satisfy.

\subsection{Design}

The dataset contains \nDataNsamples\ accepted simulations over \nDataNmaterials\
materials and \nDataNconditions\ operating conditions, sampled by a scrambled Sobol
sequence over eight material parameters and three condition parameters (Figure~\ref{fig:dataset});
\nDataNrejected\ candidates were rejected by a screen before being solved. The
kinetic coefficient is not sampled independently: it is derived from particle size
through a Glueckauf relation \cite{glueckauf1947theory,glueckauf1955theory}, so kinetics
and geometry stay physically consistent. The later of those two papers is closed at the
publisher and was not read; the linear-driving-force factor is verified through Perry's
handbook \cite{levan2019adsorption}, and the earlier paper is cited because the plain
factor is attributed to it (Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}).

Damk\"ohler number is reported with its denominator attached, because the two useful
summaries differ, and either, quoted unlabelled, cannot be reconciled with the
other. \emph{Per sample} (\nDataNsamples\ values): median
\nDaSampleMedian, range \nDaSampleMin--\nDaSampleMax, \nDaSampleBand\,\% in the
informative band (Damk\"ohler 5 to 60, the band declared in the L6 pre-registration). \emph{Per material} (\nDataNmaterials\ values, each the median
over that material's own conditions, and the axis that rung L6's slope test
regresses on): median \nDaMatMedian, range \nDaMatMin--\nDaMatMax, spanning \nDaMatDecades{}
decades, \nDaMatBand\,\% in band.

That range is the reason this dataset exists. An earlier dataset had a per-sample
median of \nDaLegacyMedian\ and placed \nDaLegacyAbove\,\% of its samples
\emph{above} the band, which made the primary hypothesis of rung L6 (that
separating the kinetic object should pay most where kinetics dominate) untestable
in principle rather than merely underpowered. That figure had itself lived only in a
comment in the generator until it was recomputed for this paper
(Section~\ref{sec:corrections}, B63); it reproduces exactly.

\subsection{The splits, and why the test is clustered}

\paragraph{The statistics in plain terms} Several breakthrough fields share one
material, so they are not independent observations; we therefore resample whole
materials, never individual fields. We set each significance threshold so that, in
simulations where no real effect exists, the test falsely claims one only rarely, at
the rate stated below for each design. For every ``no difference'' we state the
smallest effect the test could have detected, and where a model was chosen as the
best of several on the test materials we widen its interval to pay for that choice.

Materials are held out, not conditions. Every five-fold rung on this dataset (L1, L2,
L6, L7 and the re-measurements of L5) reports over the same partition of the
\nNclustFolds\ materials, so those rungs are compared over the same held-out set in the
same folds. L4's material axis uses the dataset's single split of \nLfourbMatNmat\
held-out materials, and its time axis scores a held-out window on that split's
\nLfourbTimeNmat\ training materials.

Errors are strongly clustered by material, and a test that ignores that
over-rejects. Measured under a realistic null, the percentile cluster bootstrap
rejects \nFprLegacyNominal\,\% of the time at a nominal 5\,\% level with
\nNclustLegacy\ clusters, and \nFprFoldsNominal\,\% with \nNclustFolds. We therefore
use a calibrated level: \(\alpha = \nAlphaFolds\) at \nNclustFolds\ clusters, which
achieves an empirical size of \nFprFoldsCal\,\%. One published verdict did not
survive that calibration and was withdrawn (Section~\ref{sec:corrections}, A13).

\paragraph{Three designs, their levels, all stated} Rungs L1, L2, L6 and L7, the
cost accounting, the re-measurement of L5's basis-versus-map decomposition and L5's
DeepONet basis-size sweep run on the
\nNclustFolds-material folds at \(\alpha = \nAlphaFolds\). Rung L4 runs on the single
split (\nLfourbMatNmat\ held-out materials on its material axis) at \(\alpha = \nAlphaManifest\)
(empirical size \nFprManifestCal\,\%). Rung L3,
L5's operator comparisons, the first measurement of its decomposition and the
coordinate change run on the earlier \nNclustLegacy-material design and report at \(\alpha = \nLfiveAlpha\),
which achieves an empirical size of \nFprLegacyUsed\,\% there, stricter than the
\(\alpha = \nAlphaLegacySel\) the calibration selected for that design. The nominal
levels mislead if compared directly: the smaller design's is a quarter of the larger
design's, but its empirical size is \emph{higher} (\nFprLegacyUsed\,\% against
\nFprFoldsCal\,\%), because the percentile bootstrap over-rejects more at fewer
clusters. Both are given so that each interval can be traced to the test that
produced it.

The smaller design has a cost: it supports the weakest statistical
statement in the paper. At \nNclustLegacy\ clusters the design's minimum detectable
effect at 80\,\% power is \nLegacyMDE\,\%: a ``no difference'' verdict from it
excludes only very large effects, and the calibration script reports exactly this.
Where a null is reported on that design below, the interval itself is quoted in
relative terms, because the observed paired interval is much tighter than the
design-level figure: the arms being compared are trained on the same data and
their errors are correlated, which the generic power simulation cannot know.
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} says what else follows.

\subsection{Every null carries its power or its interval}

A null verdict without a power statement is not a result; this project learned
it from its own error: an audit reported one of our nulls as well powered, at 96\,\%
power against a 20\,\% effect, and the recomputation with material-level clustering
preserved gave seven per cent (A22). A second correction, in the opposite direction,
found that the corrected power simulation itself inflated within-material noise by
\(\sqrt{2}\) (A25). Both are in Section~\ref{sec:corrections}. Every null reported
below carries either a minimum detectable effect, computed by a single, self-tested
implementation from the real paired differences, or the range of effects its interval
admits.

\subsection*{Parameter space}

Materials and operating conditions are sampled separately and crossed,
\nNclustFolds\ materials at \nDcNcond\ conditions each, so that a material is a real

\subsection*{Error metric and statistical test}

The primary metric is a per-sample normalised root-mean-square error on the
concentration channel. For sample \(s\), with \(\hat c_{ij}\) and \(c_{ij}\) the
predicted and reference \(c/c_{\mathrm{in}}\) on the rung's evaluation grid,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{nRMSE}_c(s) = \frac{\Bigl[\frac{1}{N_zN_t}\sum_{i,j}(\hat c_{ij}-c_{ij})^2\Bigr]^{1/2}}
{\max_{i,j} c_{ij} - \min_{i,j} c_{ij}} ,
\label{eq:nrmse}
\end{equation}
the RMSE over the whole space--time field divided by that sample's own reference
range. Every accepted run starts from a clean bed and ends with the outlet near the
feed concentration, so the denominator is close to one and cannot vanish. Where only
part of the field is scored (the time axis of rung L4) the numerator runs over the
scored window and the denominator stays the range of the full field. The same quantity
is computed for \(q/q_{\max}\) and \(T/T_{\mathrm{in}}\) and reported as secondary; the
channels are never pooled. Rung L7 scores the outlet curve only. Per-sample errors are
averaged over the training seeds, pooled across held-out folds so that each sample
appears once, and averaged with equal weight per sample.

Two arms are compared on identical samples by a paired, cluster-robust percentile
bootstrap: materials are resampled with replacement, all conditions of each drawn
material are kept, and the mean paired difference is recomputed. A difference is
reported as significant only if the percentile interval excludes zero, at a level
calibrated per design: simulating clustered nulls with each design's real
conditions-per-material counts, we take the largest nominal level on a fixed grid
whose empirical false-positive rate does not exceed the target. This gives
\(\alpha = \nAlphaFolds\) for the five-fold design (empirical size \nFprFoldsCal\,\%);
on the legacy design we report at the stricter \(\alpha = \nLfiveAlpha\) (empirical
size \nFprLegacyUsed\,\%). The time axis of rung L4 has an intermediate number of
clusters, at which no calibration was run; it uses the level both neighbouring
designs select. Rung L6's primary estimand is the least-squares slope of the
per-material paired difference on \(\log_{10}\mathrm{Da}\), with a material bootstrap
interval. Best-of-grid selection is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection*{Surrogates, encodings and evaluation designs}

Three evaluation designs are used, and each result states which. In the
\emph{five-fold} design the \nNclustFolds\ materials of the larger dataset are
partitioned into five folds, every material is held out once, and each fit sees the
other four folds. The \emph{single split} holds out the dataset's own
\nLfourbMatNmat\ test materials. The \emph{legacy} design holds out \nNclustLegacy\
of the earlier dataset's \nDcNmatLeg\ materials. Every learned arm is trained with
three seeds and fitted (including any POD basis and any
scaler) on its fitting set only, which is asserted disjoint from the held-out
materials before every fit.

Absolute errors are not comparable across rungs. The rungs differ in
dataset, in field encoding, in what is scored (the whole field, a time window or the
outlet curve) and in held-out set. Only paired comparisons within a rung are
inferential, and Table~\ref{tab:rungs} states each rung's encoding so that no
cross-rung reading is made by accident. The one exception is stated where it is used
(Section~\ref{sec:warp}): L5's operators and the coordinate change share a split, an
encoding and a metric, so their absolute errors are comparable.

\subsection{L1: more data}

\paragraph{The arms} Pooled out of fold over 240 held-out materials, with 64
POD modes per channel, the multilayer perceptron is the
best fixed-basis arm at \nLoneMLP, ahead of gradient boosting at \nLoneXGB, random
forest at \nLoneRF\ and ridge at \nLoneRidge. Against gradient boosting the
difference is \nLoneMLPvsXGBdiff\ with confidence interval
[\nLoneMLPvsXGBlo, \nLoneMLPvsXGBhi]; it is significant, and the perceptron is ahead in
every one of the five folds. This reverses the earlier result at 48
materials, where the three nonlinear arms were indistinguishable and the perceptron
had the worst point estimate.

The floor moved too. The POD floor is \nLonePodFloor,
and the best arm sits \(\nLoneXfloor\times\) above it. On the smaller dataset the same comparison gave \nLoneLegacyXfloorMin--\nLoneLegacyXfloorMax\(\times\), against \(\nLoneXfloor\times\) now, so \emph{the basis is even less the constraint than it was}: 192 training materials instead of 48 improved the arm, and improved the floor beneath it by more.

\paragraph{The learning curve, and the rung's actual verdict} The curve is measured
with a lighter learner than the arms above, so that every size can be run with three
seeds: the concentration channel only, \nLCpField\ POD modes and one gradient-boosted
regressor per mode, the encoding of the legacy curve it is compared with. Its absolute
errors are therefore not those of the perceptron above; its slope is the result.
Held-out error (Figure~\ref{fig:rungs}B) falls
\nLCsmallest\ \(\to\) \nLClargest\ as training materials go 12 \(\to\) 192, a factor
of \nLCratio. Every consecutive step is a significant improvement \emph{including
the last}: 96 \(\to\) 192 gives \nLClastDiff\ with interval
[\nLClastLo, \nLClastHi]. In the number of training materials \(N_{\mathrm{mat}}\) the
error falls as \(N_{\mathrm{mat}}^{-\nLCbeta}\), exponent interval
[\nLCbetaLo, \nLCbetaHi]. Extrapolated at that exponent (\emph{outside} the range
we claim, and labelled an extrapolation because the claim below excludes it),
halving the error would cost about \(\nLCmaterialsForHalving\times\) more materials.

\emph{Verdict: the materials axis is not eliminated at the largest size
available.} We claim no extrapolation beyond twice the measured
range.

\paragraph{What more materials buy, and what they do not} Refitting the basis on
each subset changes nothing: \nLCsmallestFixed\ against \nLCsmallest\ at 12
materials, \nLClargestFixed\ against \nLClargest\ at 192. A basis fitted on 12
materials reconstructs held-out fields as well as one fitted on 192, once the
regressor sees the same data. \emph{None of the gain is in the basis; all of it is
in the parameter-to-coefficient map.} Modes whose coefficient map clears
a fifth of the variance grow from \nLCmodesSmall\ to \nLCmodesLarge\ and are still climbing (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}).

The conditions axis behaves differently: exponent \nLCcondBeta, \nLCexpRatio{} of the material
exponent. Both axes are open; materials are the one that matters.

\paragraph{The regressor class} If the map binds, a different regressor class on the
same coefficients might bind less. The first pre-registered baseline replaces the perceptron with a Gaussian process on
the same POD coefficients, folds and materials (kernel hyperparameters fitted on a
subset of each fold, by a pre-registration amendment made before any baseline ran,
because a full marginal-likelihood fit would have taken hundreds of hours of
computation). \emph{Verdict: \nBgpVerdict}: \nBgpGp\ against \nBgpMlp, interval
[\nBgpLo, \nBgpHi]; the design detects a \nBgpMde\,\% effect with \(80\,\%\) power. Two
very different function classes reach the same error from the same per-mode inputs:
for a map fitted mode by mode, the regressor class is not the lever. How the map is
structured is the lever: training the basis and the coefficients together, or learning the
whole field, is worth a constant factor (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}).

\subsection{L2: more capacity}

\nLtwoNconfigs{} configurations in both directions around the L1 setting, on the same
folds and basis. \nLtwoNUshaped{} sweeps are U-shaped with interior optima
(width \nLtwoWidthBest{} for the perceptron's width, \nLtwoDepthBest{} layers for its depth, tree
depth \nLtwoXgbBest{} for gradient boosting) and the random forest
\nLtwoRfShape: it is still improving at its last step, but that step is a fully
grown forest, the ceiling of its capacity knob. The decisive evidence is decoupling:
gradient boosting drops its training error by more than an order of magnitude across
the depth sweep while held-out error passes through a minimum and then rises.

\emph{Verdict: capacity is not eliminated.} The rule
eliminates it only if every family has saturated, and one has not: the random forest
is still improving at its last step (fully grown trees, \nLtwoRfBest; last-step
interval [\nLtwoRfLastLo, \nLtwoRfLastHi]). The rule then asks for the sweep to be
extended, and it cannot be: one sample per leaf is the ceiling of that family's
capacity. The verdict therefore stays as declared. Our interpretation, which is
not part of the verdict: the neural and boosting families saturate with interior
optima, and the one family that has not run out of improvement has run out of
capacity while sitting well behind the best perceptron. Either way, the optimum is not where the
earlier sweep left it. The best configuration anywhere is \nLtwoBestName\
at \nLtwoBest, which is \(\nLtwoVsLonePct\,\%\) better than the best L1 arm, the
perceptron (interval \(\nLtwoVsLoneLo\)--\(\nLtwoVsLoneHi\,\%\); adjusted for having
picked the best of \nLtwoSelK\ configurations on the same materials, namely the
\nLtwoNconfigs\ of the sweep less the L1 setting, which appears in it twice and is the
reference:
\(\nLtwoSelLo\)--\(\nLtwoSelHi\,\%\), Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). At 48 materials the
same comparison (best configuration against the best L1 arm, there gradient
boosting) gave \(\nLtwoLegacyVsLonePct\,\%\), with six and eight layers in a
near-tie (\nLtwoLegacyDsix{} against \nLtwoLegacyDeight) where at 192 the optimum is
eight. The earlier sentence ``capacity is irrelevant'' was scoped to a material count
without saying so, and is withdrawn as A26. The wall, the gap between the best
surrogate and the basis floor, stands: the best fixed-basis
surrogate still sits \(\nLtwoXfloor\times\) above the floor.

What changes with the material count is \emph{which} configuration is best, not that
capacity grows: the perceptron's depth optimum moves up from a near-tie to eight
layers, its width optimum moves \emph{down}, and the boosting depth moves by one step,
within a near-tie. We therefore draw no data-limited inference from L2 alone; that
diagnosis rests on the learning curve of L1.

\subsection{L3: a better basis}

At matched parameter counts and each family at its own best learning rate, the
multilayer perceptron beats both Kolmogorov--Arnold network \cite{liu2025kan} families at every budget:
\nLthreeSigPair\ of the \nLthreePairs\ pairwise comparisons are significant as pairs, and
\nLthreeSigSim\ remain significant once both learning-rate choices are paid for with one
simultaneous interval over all \nLthreeSelK\ pairs (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}); in
none is the Kolmogorov--Arnold network the better arm. \nLthreeArms\ arms were trained
across \nLthreeNrates\ learning rates spanning \nLthreeDecades\ decades, until every
Kolmogorov--Arnold optimum was bracketed on both sides. The perceptron's optimum sits at
the lowest rate, an open edge (the next rate up is worse by
\nLthreeEdgeSatMin--\nLthreeEdgeSatMax\,\%); for this verdict that is conservative,
because better tuning could only widen the perceptron's lead. Every family was tuned
over the same grid, and the families' optima lie \nLthreeLrDecades{} orders of
magnitude apart (Supplementary S2, Section S3). In a separate audit configuration (a
fixed budget, depth three), the best Kolmogorov--Arnold network in the study is the
\emph{least} Kolmogorov--Arnold-like one and reaches \nLthreeBestKan\ against the
perceptron's \nLthreeBestKanMlp\ (difference \nLthreeBestKanDiff, per-pair interval
[\nLthreeBestKanLo, \nLthreeBestKanHi]).

\paragraph{The literature is split} Shukla and co-workers
\cite{shukla2024comprehensive} find spline-based Kolmogorov--Arnold networks less
accurate and efficient than perceptrons, and polynomial variants comparable but not
robust; Wang and co-workers \cite{wang2025kinn} and Rigas and co-workers
\cite{rigas2026training} report Kolmogorov--Arnold architectures that outperform
perceptron baselines on solid-mechanics and physics-informed problems; and Kiyani and
co-workers \cite{kiyani2025optimizing} find that optimiser choice dominates accuracy for
both families, which is why every competitor optimum here is bracketed. A
Kolmogorov--Arnold operator is not new either: DeepOKAN \cite{abueidda2025deepokan}
uses the same Gaussian radial basis, and our claim for that arm is the application to
column-scale transfer (A12). The accurate statement is narrow: on \emph{this} problem,
at matched parameters, the perceptron wins every comparison by point estimate.

\paragraph{Interpretation} The result is not that Kolmogorov--Arnold networks are bad.
At a fixed budget, parameters spent on per-edge basis richness buy training fit but not
generalisation, while the same parameters spent on width buy both: the
Kolmogorov--Arnold arms fit the training set as well as or better than the perceptron
at every budget and are worse on held-out materials every time.

\subsection{L4: the residual as a loss, and physics at inference}

\paragraph{Residual formulation} The residual of the first run advected the gas at the
superficial velocity \(v\) instead of the interstitial \(v/\varepsilon_t\) the solver
integrates (B72): on the solver's own fields it left \nBsevtwoOldResid\,\% of the
adsorption term unexplained, against \nBsevtwoNewResid\,\% for the solver's equation.
Every result of that run was withdrawn (A28), a gate now checks the training residual
against the solver's coefficients, and the rung was re-run in full with the corrected
residual (equations~\eqref{eq:mass}--\eqref{eq:energy} with \eqref{eq:danckwerts}) and
the same design, seeds and verdict words. Every number below is from the re-run.

\paragraph{Design} Each weighting scheme is compared with a data-only twin at matched
architecture, optimiser steps and supervised budget: fixed weights over six decades,
gradient-norm balancing at three target ratios \cite{wang2021understanding},
neural-tangent-kernel weighting \cite{wang2022when} (per-point gradient norms as a trace
estimate) and self-adaptive per-point weights \cite{mcclenny2023self}. An arm whose
seen-window error exceeds three times the twin's has abandoned the data fit and is
excluded as failed-to-train (\nLfourbNabandoned\ on the time axis,
\nLfourbNabandonedMat\ on the material axis). On both axes the best arm is an interior
fixed weight, so the edge rule is not engaged.

\paragraph{Time axis: no difference} \emph{Verdict: there is no difference at the
best weighting found.} The best physics arm reaches \nLfourbTimeBest\ against the
twin's \nLfourbTimeTwin\ (interval [\nLfourbTimeLo, \nLfourbTimeHi], \nLfourbTimeNmat\
materials; minimum detectable effect \nLfourbTimeMDE\,\%), so the legacy claim that the
residual hurts is withdrawn. After a pre-registered L-BFGS polish
\cite{rathore2024challenges} the twin improves more (\nLfourbTimePolTwin\ against
\nLfourbTimePolBest, interval [\nLfourbTimePolLo, \nLfourbTimePolHi]): under the
second-order optimiser the data-only arm is significantly better on this axis.

\paragraph{Material axis: the residual helps, at small weights only} \emph{Verdict:
physics helps once correctly weighted.} A fixed weight of \(10^{\nLfourbBestWeightExp}\)
reaches \nLfourbMatBest\ against the twin's \nLfourbMatTwin\ on \nLfourbMatNmat\
held-out materials (interval [\nLfourbMatLo, \nLfourbMatHi]), a gain of
\nLfourbMatGainPct\,\%; the decades either side are also better than the twin, while
every larger fixed weight and every adaptive scheme is significantly worse
(\nLfourbNworseMat\ of \nLfourbNeligibleMat\ eligible arms). The best arm was chosen on
the test materials, so we recompute its interval to hold for every eligible arm at once
(a studentised max-statistic cluster bootstrap, critical value \nLfourbSelQ\ standard

excluding no difference. A small residual weight is a real but weak regulariser for
transfer, the weight must itself be searched for, and larger weights cost accuracy.

\paragraph{Physics at inference: worse, even with the right equation} Test-time
refinement minimises the residual for each held-out unit without data. On the time axis
its pre-registered anchor failed (the seen-window error rose \nLfourbTimeSeenRise\,\%,
above the \(10\,\%\) limit), so the material axis carries the result: refinement takes
the twin's held-out error from \nLfourbRefTwinBefore\ to \nLfourbRefTwinAfter, about
\nLfourbRefTwinFactor{} times worse (interval [\nLfourbRefTwinLo, \nLfourbRefTwinHi]),
and the best physics arm's from \nLfourbRefBestBefore\ to \nLfourbRefBestAfter. With the
solver's own equation, minimising the residual without data moves a good prediction
away from the truth; we report this as a measurement and do not claim its cause.

\subsection{L5: does an operator remove the wall?}

\paragraph{Basis size, on the full design: a small gain, complete early} The
pre-registered re-measurement on the \nNclustFolds-material five-fold design
(with two amendments made before any verdict was computed: a tripled step budget,
triggered by the protocol's own budget rule after the first sweep, and an interval that
pays for the learning-rate choice; B74) answers the rung's primary question. \emph{Verdict: \nLfiveVtwoVerdict}. DeepONet at \(p = 128\) reaches
\nLfiveVtwoPhigh\ against \nLfiveVtwoPeight\ at \(p = 8\), interval
[\nLfiveVtwoDiffLo, \nLfiveVtwoDiffHi]; paid for over both learning-rate choices the
interval is [\nLfiveVtwoSelLo, \nLfiveVtwoSelHi] (\nLfiveVtwoSelWords). The gain is
\nLfiveVtwoGainPct\,\% of the error, and by point estimate it is complete by \(p = 16\)
(\nLfiveVtwoPsixteen); the larger bases are level. At \(p = 8\) DeepONet sits only
\(\nLfiveVtwoXfloorEight\times\) above its own basis floor, so there the basis does
bind; by \(p = 128\) it sits \(\nLfiveVtwoXfloorHigh\times\) above it, and the map
binds. The legacy verdict below is retracted as a small-design artefact (A29).

\paragraph{On the legacy design (retracted as a verdict; kept as a measurement)} At
matched parameter counts DeepONet looked flat in basis size on the legacy design
(Figure~\ref{fig:operators}): \nLfiveFlatDiff\ from \(p = 8\) to \(p = 128\), interval
[\nLfiveFlatLo, \nLfiveFlatHi]: a sixteenfold larger basis could have improved this arm
by at most \nLfiveFlatImproveMax\,\% of its error. The larger design then found an
improvement well inside that bound, which is why a null on \nNclustLegacy\ held-out
materials could not be the last word (A29). \emph{The best DeepONet found anywhere,
at the smallest basis, is as accurate as an oracle handed the true coefficients of
its first \nLfiveOracleONet\ modes.} The tuning checks are in Supplementary S2,
Section S4.

\paragraph{The arm the theory names} Lanthaler and co-workers \cite{lanthaler2023nonlinear} prove that operator
architectures with a linear reconstruction step (DeepONet, PCA-Net, a principal-component network, and
POD-plus-regressor) are lower-bounded on
advection-dominated problems, and that the Fourier neural operator escapes that
bound through nonlinear reconstruction. Our original rung tested only the family the
theory condemns; that deviation from our own frozen protocol is recorded as B33. The
prediction was pre-registered before the run.

The Fourier neural operator is more accurate, \nLfiveFNO\ against DeepONet's
\nLfiveONet, but not significantly once its selection is paid for. As a pair fixed in
advance the difference of \nLfiveONetFNOdiff\ has interval [\nLfiveONetFNOlo,
\nLfiveONetFNOhi]; but both arms are the best of grids chosen on the same
\nNclustLegacy\ held-out materials (\nLfiveSelNfno\ Fourier and \nLfiveSelNonet\
DeepONet cells), and paying for both choices over all \nLfiveSelK\ pairs (critical value
\nLfiveSelQ\ standard errors) widens it to [\nLfiveSelLo, \nLfiveSelHi], which includes
zero (B75). What survives is the direction the theory predicts. It does not remove the
gap: the Fourier operator sits \(\nFNOxFloor\times\) above the linear-reconstruction
floor at \(p = 128\), where DeepONet sits \(\nONetxFloor\times\), so its
\(\nFNOgain\times\) improvement is a dent, not an escape (the floor is a reference level
for the Fourier operator, not a bound).

At a matched budget the best Fourier operator is \emph{\nLfiveFNOwidth\ channels wide}
against the best DeepONet's \nLfiveONetWidth: a nonlinear reconstruction matches the
best linear one with a fraction of the channels. Reconstruction is not what separates
the families in general, though: POD with a pointwise regressor and DeepONet both
reconstruct linearly and differ by \(\nONetLever\times\) (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}),
so most of the learner lever is training the basis and the coefficients together. The
Fourier operator's mode sweep is confounded with width at a matched budget (its
spectral weights cost \(\mathcal{O}(w^2 m^2)\)), so basis-size statements are made for
DeepONet only.

\subsection{L6: separate identification of the kinetic and equilibrium objects}

This was the project's primary hypothesis: separating the kinetic and equilibrium
objects should pay most where kinetics dominate. Both invalidation checks passed before
any verdict was computed: the kinetic object is unrecoverable from observable
descriptors (\(R^2 = \nLsixKineticRtwo\)) while the equilibrium shape is recoverable
(\(R^2 = \nLsixStepRtwo\) for the step position, \nLsixQmaxRtwo\ for the capacity),
the asymmetry the hypothesis needs.

\paragraph{Primary estimand: not supported, and a weak null} The separate-versus-joint
difference shows no detectable dependence on Damk\"ohler number: slope \nLsixSlope\
per decade, interval [\nLsixSlopeLo, \nLsixSlopeHi], Pearson \(r = \nLsixSlopeR\),
over \nLsixDaDecades\ decades of per-material Damk\"ohler number
(\nDaMatMin--\nDaMatMax; \nDaMatBand\,\% of materials inside the informative band;
Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}B). \emph{Verdict: the mechanism is not supported.} The null
is weak: across the tested range the upper end of the slope interval allows a change of
\nLsixSlopeSpanMax\ nRMSE, about \nLsixSlopeVsEffect\ times the main effect below, so a
dependence as large as the benefit itself is not ruled out. Part of the axis's spread
is also run length, for the \nDcExtendedPct\,\% of runs whose horizon was extended
(Section~\ref{sec:model}).

\paragraph{The scope of that refutation} The dataset never reaches kinetic
control: its lowest per-material Damk\"ohler number is \nDaMatMin, and reaching unity
would need pellets of about \nDaPelletForUnity\,\si{\milli\metre}, beyond a
one-dimensional model of this bed. The refutation holds across the range a physically
consistent bed of this kind can occupy, not in the kinetically controlled limit
(Supplementary S2, Section S5).

\paragraph{Secondary estimand: significant} Joint fitting gives \nLsixJoint\ and
separate identification \nLsixSeparate: separate is better by \nLsixDiff, interval
[\nLsixLo, \nLsixHi], consistent in all five folds and all three seeds (on the legacy
dataset, which could not test the estimand, the sign was reversed and not
significant).

\paragraph{Why the test has power now} Sobol sampling and physically consistent
kinetics cut the between-material noise \(\nLsixNoiseDrop\times\); with
\nNclustFolds\ clusters the minimum detectable effect at 80\,\% power is \nLsixMDE\,\%,
and the observed effect sits just under it, so the lower bound is the conservative
reading (Supplementary S2, Section S6).

\paragraph{Verdict} \emph{Structural decomposition gives a
small but real and reproducible improvement in transfer to unseen materials, with no
detectable dependence on Damk\"ohler number.} The pattern is that of an inductive
bias rather than a kinetic identifier, which is not what the hypothesis predicted;
since a kinetic component of the benefit's own size is not excluded, this is the
better-supported reading rather than an established one.

\subsection{L7: is learning needed at all?}

Arms are scored like for like on the outlet curve, because the closed forms predict a curve and
not a field. The learned arm's out-of-fold prediction reaches \nLsevenLearned{}
against \nLsevenKlinkenberg\ for the best closed form, Klinkenberg's approximation
\cite{klinkenberg1948numerical,klinkenberg1954heat}, \nLsevenShock\ for
equilibrium theory and \nLsevenPattern\ for constant-pattern theory. The best closed
form is \nLsevenRatio{}\(\times\) worse, with difference \nLsevenDiff\ and interval
[\nLsevenLo, \nLsevenHi]. \emph{Verdict: learning is justified} on the larger
dataset: every classical form is significantly worse than the learned reference on
the outlet curve.

\paragraph{Fitted closed forms} Scoring the closed forms with nothing
fitted is the strict test, not the fair one. The third pre-registered baseline gives the Klinkenberg form two physically interpretable
corrections (multipliers on its Henry slope and its rate constant), fitted on each
fold's training materials and applied unchanged to the held-out ones. It improves
markedly, to \nBfitFitted, and is still \(\nBfitRatio\times\) worse than the learned
arm's \nBfitLearned\ (interval [\nBfitLo, \nBfitHi]). \emph{Verdict: \nBfitWords}.

The transferable statement is that a classical model wins exactly when the physics
it assumes is the physics that is there. Where a problem is linear and time-invariant, a Green's function is the exact
solution operator and a linear convolution is the right model, not an approximation
to one. Adsorption with an inflected
isotherm is nonlinear, and the two-wave structure violates all three closed-form
assumptions: a linear isotherm, a non-spreading favourable front, and infinitely
fast kinetics.

\section{What binds the error}
\label{sec:mechanism}

\begin{figure}[!tbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../figures/Fig5_mechanism.pdf}
\caption{What binds the error: the learned map, mode by mode. Full design, the
learning-curve learner (\nLCpField\ POD modes of \(c\), one gradient-boosted regressor
per mode). \textbf{A}~Held-out \(R^2\) of the map from material parameters to each of
the first POD coefficients, one curve per number of training materials: the leading
modes are learned first, and each increase in training materials lifts nearly every
mode. \textbf{B}~The number of modes whose map reaches \(R^2 > 0.2\) (solid) and
\(R^2 > 0.5\) (dashed) against the number of training materials, still rising at the
largest size affordable.}
\label{fig:mechanism}
\end{figure}

Most models here build a breakthrough field as a weighted sum of stored patterns (POD
with a regressor, and DeepONet); the Fourier operator learns the field directly. A
model of the first kind can fail in exactly two ways: the basis cannot represent the
field, or the map from parameters to coefficients cannot be learned. For a surrogate
whose prediction \(\hat u\) lies in the span of an orthonormal basis
\(\{\phi_k\}_{k \le p}\), such as POD with a regressor, the error of each sample splits
exactly, because the two terms are orthogonal:
\begin{equation}
\lVert u - \hat u \rVert^2 \;=\; \underbrace{\lVert u - \Pi_p u \rVert^2}_{\text{basis}}
\;+\; \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{k \le p} \bigl(c_k - \hat c_k\bigr)^2}_{\text{map}},
\qquad c_k = \langle u, \phi_k \rangle ,
\label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
in the discrete norm of the stored grid, with \(\Pi_p\) the orthogonal projection onto
the basis. The first term is the basis floor; the second is the error of the learned
map. An oracle handed the exact first \(k\) coefficients calibrates any learner,
including those whose output is not in that span, as an equivalent mode count; for those
learners the floor is a reference level rather than a bound. We work in the
\emph{optimal} basis, so the answer cannot depend on the basis a particular method
learned.

\paragraph{The map binds} On the legacy design the basis error falls from
\nLfiveBasisSmall\ to \nLfiveBasisLarge\ as \(p\) goes from 8 to 128, a factor of
\(\nFloorDrop\), while the coefficient-map error (gradient boosting, the better
regressor at every basis size) moves from \nLfiveCoefSmall\ to \nLfiveCoefLarge, a
change of \nLfiveCoefChangePct\,\%. The reason is that on held-out materials the map
predicts few modes: \nLfiveCountModes\ clear \(R^2 > 0.2\) (the leading
\nLfiveLeadModes\ and \nLfiveIsolatedModes\ isolated later ones), and the median \(R^2\)
of the \nLfiveTailModes\ modes after the leading run is \(\nLfiveTailMedian\), worse than
predicting the mean. Against the oracle, the best DeepONet performs like a
\nLfiveOracleONet-mode oracle, the best Fourier operator like a
\nLfiveOracleFNO-mode one, DeepOKAN like a \nLfiveOracleOKAN-mode one and POD with
gradient boosting like a \nLfiveOraclePodReg-mode one. Training the basis and the
coefficients together, or learning the whole field at once, is worth up to
\(\nOperatorLever\times\) from the pointwise regressor to the Fourier operator (point
estimates between best-of-grid arms), comparable to the learning curve's gain; it does
not close the distance to the floor. On the legacy design the most accurate learner by
point estimate still sits \(\nFNOxFloor\times\) above it.

\paragraph{The decomposition holds on the full design} Re-measured where every other
rung reports (\nLfiveVtwoNmat\ materials, five folds, three seeds;
Figure~\ref{fig:operators}B), the basis floor falls from \nLfiveVtwoFloorSmall\ to
\nLfiveVtwoFloorLarge, a factor of \(\nLfiveVtwoFloorDrop\), while the coefficient-map
error moves from \nLfiveVtwoCoefSmall\ to \nLfiveVtwoCoefLarge, \nLfiveVtwoCoefChangePct\,\%
of itself (computed before rounding; interval [\nLfiveVtwoCoefLo, \nLfiveVtwoCoefHi]):
statistically resolved and immaterial. At the largest basis the map predicts about
\nLfiveVtwoModes\ modes on held-out materials (the learning-curve learner, on its own
\nLCpField-mode basis, clears \nLCmodesLarge\ at the same 192 training materials;
Supplementary S2, Section S7), and DeepONet sits
\(\nLfiveVtwoXfloorHigh\times\) above the floor.

\paragraph{The mode count is scoped to a sample size} The number of modes a learner
predicts is not a property of the map but of the training set: modes clearing
\(R^2 > 0.2\) grow from \nLCmodesSmall\ to \nLCmodesLarge\ over 12 to 192 materials
and are still climbing (A21; Supplementary S2, Section S7).

\paragraph{The \(n\)-width is real and was not what binds} The Kolmogorov \(n\)-width,
the smallest error any \(n\)-dimensional linear basis can achieve, is estimated here by
the POD residual, which for this snapshot family decays as \(k^{\nNwidthExpErr}\) in the
number of modes \(k\), so halving the
basis error requires \(\nNwidthHalving\times\) the modes; any method whose output is a
linear combination of a fixed basis is bounded below by it. Our frozen protocol
predicted that DeepONet would track this bound; it does not, because the map binds
first (A17). The decay is faster than the \(\tfrac12 k^{-1/2}\) lower bound Ohlberger
and Rave \cite{ohlberger2016reduced} prove for linear advection with jump
discontinuities, because axial dispersion and finite-rate kinetics smooth our fronts
(Supplementary S2, Section S7).

\subsection{Scope and prior art}

Aligning multiple transports is not new, and none of it is claimed here. The
two-transport case is the founding example of the shifted POD \cite{reiss2018shifted},
multi-front variants follow
\cite{nair2019transported,mendible2020dimensionality,krah2025robust,krah2023front,zorawski2024neural},
a neural-network shifted decomposition predates the last of these
\cite{papapicco2022nnspod}, and breakthrough curves have been decomposed with a single
characteristic time \cite{lee2026scaleup}. Our two-landmark linear warp is piecewise
landmark registration from functional data analysis \cite{kneip1992statistical} (a
landmark-free alternative is in \cite{ramsay1998curve}); Zucatti and Zahr
\cite{zucatti2026model} apply the same primitive to shock features in reduced-order
models of convection-dominated problems, and registration-based reduction is an
established family \cite{taddei2020registration,taddei2026erratum}. What is ours is the
measurement.

\subsection{One shift makes it worse; two make it much better}

The standard remedy for a transport-dominated \(n\)-width is to factor the front
position out before projecting \cite{reiss2018shifted}. We warped each field onto its own local arrival time
and swept the alignment level. \emph{It makes the \(n\)-width worse}: aligning on
the half-height crossing takes the modes needed for 99.9\,\% of training variance
from \nNwidthOriginal\ to \nNwidthSingle. It does not help accuracy even given
the exact front trajectory for free: handed the oracle warp, the co-moving frame
still loses to the fixed frame. The reason was measured before it was invoked: the separation between the two waves spans a factor of \nGapRange, and one shift cannot align
two waves whose separation varies that much.

Using \emph{two} shifts, one per wave, and normalising the interval between them (Figure~\ref{fig:warp}),
\begin{equation}
\sigma(z,t) = \frac{t - t_{\mathrm{lo}}(z)}{t_{\mathrm{hi}}(z) - t_{\mathrm{lo}}(z)},
\label{eq:warp}
\end{equation}
collapses the \(n\)-width where one shift inflated it: \nNwidthOriginal\ \(\to\)
\nNwidthTwoWaveA\ modes, or \nNwidthTwoWaveB\ for the wider landmark pair. A third
pair was excluded because a narrow window divides by a small span and its
interpolation floor reached \nComovingFloorPct\,\% of the fixed-frame error it is judged
against, above the guard's threshold of \(10\,\%\).

The predicted-front arm does not beat the fixed frame. Given the true front
trajectories, the same POD-plus-regressor arm is \nWarpHeadroomFixed{}\(\times\) better
than the fixed frame, which is what perfect front location would buy, and
\nWarpHeadroom{}\(\times\) better than with predicted fronts. On \nWarpNmat\ held-out
materials the predicted-front interval admits anything from a \nWarpPredImproveMax\,\%
improvement to a \nWarpPredDegradeMax\,\% degradation; it rules out the oracle's gain,
not a modest one. With that scope, this is a relocation of the obstruction. Through L1--L5 the obstruction was the
parameter-to-coefficient map, at the number of modes a given training set supports. It
is now ``two smooth monotone one-dimensional curves are not located accurately
enough'', a better-posed problem, with a target a front-locating model can be held
to. The oracle's gain is not unique to this route: the Fourier operator of L5 reaches a
comparable error on the same held-out materials without any oracle.

\paragraph{Monotonicity is not the lever} The predicted fronts violate the
constraints the true ones satisfy in \nWarpNonmonoLo\,\% of samples, but projecting
them onto the constraint set makes the reconstruction slightly worse
(Supplementary S2, Section S8); the remaining headroom is the front predictor's raw
accuracy.

\paragraph{Relation to constant-pattern behaviour} Equation~\eqref{eq:warp} is not
constant-pattern scaling \cite{knox2016limitations}: when the two fronts move at
different rates and their separation varies \nGapRange-fold, no single scale factor
aligns both, which is why the second landmark does the work.

\section{What the surrogate costs, and when it repays}
\label{sec:cost}

A surrogate that is never asked enough questions costs more than the solver it
replaces, and the crossing point can be computed. All costs are in
\emph{solve-equivalents}, multiples of one reference simulation at production
settings, so the ratio carries to other hardware; on ours the \nCostNSolves\ accepted
simulations took \nCostGenWall\ hours on \nCostWorkers\ workers, so one solve-equivalent
is \nCostSolveSec\ worker-seconds.

Against \(N_q\) screening queries the reference costs \(N_q\) solve-equivalents and the
surrogate its training set, its fit and \(N_q\) queries, so the two break even at
\begin{equation}
N_q^{\mathrm{be}} \;=\; \frac{N_{\mathrm{sim}} + c_{\mathrm{fit}}}{1 - r},
\qquad r \;=\; \frac{\text{cost of one query}}{\text{cost of one solve}} ,
\label{eq:breakeven}
\end{equation}
with \(N_{\mathrm{sim}}\) the training simulations and \(c_{\mathrm{fit}}\) the fit, both
in solve-equivalents. Fitting the L1 perceptron costs \nCostTrainEq\ solve-equivalents
(median of \nCostTrainSeeds\ fits), noise next to the data, and since \(r \ll 1\)
equation~\eqref{eq:breakeven} is bounded below by \(N_{\mathrm{sim}} +
c_{\mathrm{fit}}\) without timing a query at all. Measured on an idle machine, a
full-field query takes \nCostQueryMs\,ms, \nCostQuerySpeedup\ times cheaper than a
solve, so the bound and the exact break-even coincide.

\label{tab:cost}

\toprule

\midrule

\paragraph{What Table~\ref{tab:cost} says} First, the surrogate at its best accuracy
cannot repay its own construction in fewer than \nCostBreakEven\ queries against the
full-resolution solve. The full-resolution solve is, however, not the relevant
competitor. The second pre-registered baseline (A30) re-solves one sample per material
with the same solver on coarser grids. \emph{Verdict: a coarse solve at
\(N_z = \nCoarseNz\) matches the surrogate's held-out accuracy at \nCoarseSolveEq\
solve-equivalents per query}; in fact it is \(\nCoarseAccRatio\times\) more accurate
(\nCoarseErrPct\,\% normalised error against the perceptron's \nCoarseTargetPct\,\%) in
about \nCoarseWallMs\,ms (\nCoarseNfail\ of \nCoarseNsamp\ coarse solves failed and are
reported). The comparison is unpaired and made against the L1 and L2 perceptrons only,
so it is a statement about scale. On a production solve timed in the same process, the
surrogate breaks even with coarse solving only after about \nCoarseBreakEven\ queries
(a lower bound), and it remains the less accurate of the two. \emph{For this
one-dimensional column, a surrogate is justified only by query volume}: a screen of
tens of thousands of candidates, or a field needed in milliseconds inside an optimiser
or a controller. A two- or three-dimensional, multi-cycle device model, where a coarse
solve costs far more, is where the balance can turn; we have not measured it.

Second, the learning curve is a price list: going from 12 to 192 training materials
multiplies the data cost by \nCostDataRatio{}\(\times\) and divides the error by
\nLCratio{}\(\times\). The rows are priced for the curve's per-mode regressor; the L1
perceptron is already more accurate, and L2 found a configuration
\(\nLtwoVsLonePct\,\%\) better still, so a better learner shifts the whole frontier
down. The exchange rate between data and error is the transferable part.

Third, the eliminated rungs are interventions that add cost without buying accuracy: a
Kolmogorov--Arnold basis costs fit time and nothing else, and the closed forms are
cheaper but worse even when fitted. Of the levers that remain, only training materials
cost simulations; a better configuration, a small residual weight
(\nLfourbMatGainPct\,\%) or a different operator cost fit time or collocation points.
Every statement here is cost \emph{at a stated accuracy}: the reference answers exactly,
the surrogate with the held-out error in the third column.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Best-of-grid selection on the test materials} Several comparisons pit the
best of a grid (of learning rates, capacities, weightings or operator settings)
against a single reference, with the best chosen on the same held-out materials the
comparison is scored on. A per-pair interval is anti-conservative for a pair chosen after looking, so for
these we report a studentised max-statistic cluster bootstrap, the single-step form of
the procedure of Romano and Wolf \cite{romano2005stepwise}: materials are resampled
jointly across all candidate arms, each arm's deviation is scaled by its own standard
error, and the critical value is the quantile of the largest scaled deviation, so the
interval holds for every candidate at once. L2's best configuration, chosen from
\nLtwoSelK, keeps a gain of \(\nLtwoSelLo\)--\(\nLtwoSelHi\,\%\) over the L1 setting; the
corrected L4 re-run's material-axis gain survives (critical value \nLfourbSelQ\ standard
errors); in L3, \nLthreeSigSim\ of the \nLthreePairs\ comparisons remain significant and
none reverses. The correction changes one verdict: the Fourier operator's advantage over
DeepONet, paid for over all \nLfiveSelK\ pairs, is no longer significant (B75). Selecting
on a validation split inside the training materials is the protocol change we recommend
for any rerun.

\paragraph{One partial differential equation family} Everything here is measured on
one model of one process. The ladder is a method; its verdicts are not claimed
beyond adsorption columns with inflected isotherms.

\paragraph{Solver-versus-experiment and surrogate-versus-solver are different error
budgets, and we keep them apart} Section~\ref{sec:anchor} is the only
solver-versus-experiment comparison in this paper: one condition, on a bed whose
aspect ratio is \nAnchorAspect, where a one-dimensional plug-flow model is at the edge of its
validity. Every other number is surrogate-versus-solver.

\paragraph{The dispersion closure is evaluated at a limit} Our closure is the
high-particle-P\'eclet limit of Edwards and Richardson's correlation
\cite{edwards1968gas}, a form due to Wakao and Funazkri \cite{wakao1978effect} that
Perry's handbook \cite{levan2019adsorption} presents as a lower bound for nonporous
particles. Across the dataset the full and truncated forms differ by a median factor
of \nDispRatioMed; on the anchor bed, at a particle P\'eclet number
\(d_p u/D_m\) of \nAnchorPePart\ (\(u\), interstitial velocity; \(D_m\), molecular
diffusivity), using the full form instead removes \nAnchorTcutPct\,\% of the model's excess
95\,\% arrival time in a labelled post-hoc sensitivity (Supplementary S2, Section S9
and Figure S2). No surrogate result depends on it, because every arm sees the same
reference.

\paragraph{The isotherm and the parameter space} The low-humidity branch holds too
little water, as the anchor shows; part of the sampled heat range is occupied by no
placed framework; \nChemBelowStepPct\,\% of samples are fed below their step; the step
does not move with feed temperature as it does in real MOF-303
\cite{hastings2024high}; pellet density and bed porosity are packing values rather
than measurements; and no covalent organic framework (COF) breakthrough case suitable
for comparison exists. Each is quantified in Supplementary S2, Section S9.

\paragraph{Three comparisons are exploratory} L3, L5's operator comparison and the
coordinate change are measured over \nNclustLegacy\ held-out materials, L4 over
\nLfourbMatNmat, and L1, L2, L6 and L7 over \nNclustFolds. A verdict measured at one
material count need not survive another (A26), so the three small-design verdicts are
exploratory and scoped to that count. L3's ordering has the same sign at every budget;
against the Gaussian radial-basis family at the two larger budgets it is not
significant once selection is paid for. For L5 the re-measurement on the larger design
was made, and the legacy flat-in-basis null did not survive (A29). For the coordinate
change it is not possible as designed: the pre-registered landmark screen fails on every
fold of the larger dataset, because a landmark placed relative to each cell's own
plateau falls within two samples of the start in at least \nWarpScreenBoundaryMin\,\% of
cells on every one of the \nWarpScreenNfolds\ folds, so that result stays scoped to the
smaller design.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We asked whether the error of a parametric surrogate of an adsorption column on unseen
materials is set by its basis or by its map, as a ladder of pre-registered tests rather
than as a search for the best-fitting network.

\paragraph{What binds} The map from material parameters to the field binds, not the
basis it is expressed in. Refining the optimal basis \(\nLfiveVtwoFloorDrop\times\)
changes a per-mode map's error by \nLfiveVtwoCoefChangePct\,\%, and a basis refitted on
more materials buys nothing once the regressor sees the same data; DeepONet gains
\nLfiveVtwoGainPct\,\% from basis size, all of it by \(p = 16\) by point estimate. At the
largest basis DeepONet sits \(\nLfiveVtwoXfloorHigh\times\) above the POD floor on the
full design, and on the legacy design the most accurate learner by point estimate, a
Fourier operator, sits \(\nFNOxFloor\times\) above it. Error still falls as
\(N_{\mathrm{mat}}^{-\nLCbeta}\) with training materials at the largest size we could
afford. On the legacy design, by point estimate, learning the whole field rather than
each coefficient separately is worth up to \(\nOperatorLever\times\), of the same order
as the \(\nLCratio\times\) that sixteen times more materials buys a fixed learner.

\paragraph{What is eliminated, and what is not} Capacity is not eliminated: a random
forest is still improving at its capacity ceiling, while the neural and
boosting families saturate. At matched parameters a perceptron beats both
Kolmogorov--Arnold families in every comparison by point estimate, \nLthreeSigSim\ of
\nLthreePairs\ significantly after selection (exploratory). The Kolmogorov \(n\)-width
is real and inactive (A17). Separate equilibrium and kinetic heads help by \nLsixDiff\
nRMSE with no detectable dependence on Damk\"ohler number, which reads as an inductive
bias. The governing equations' residual as a loss improves transfer to unseen materials
by \nLfourbMatGainPct\,\% at a small weight, surviving selection, while larger or
adaptive weights hurt and residual minimisation at inference makes predictions worse: a
weak regulariser here, not a source of accuracy the data lack. A change of coordinates
that aligns the two fronts would buy \nWarpHeadroomFixed{}\(\times\) if the fronts were
known and shows no detectable gain when they are predicted (\nWarpNmat\ materials; a
gain of up to \nWarpPredImproveMax\,\% is not excluded).

\paragraph{What this means for a method developer} For a surrogate of this kind, effort
pays in the map: more and more diverse training materials, and possibly learners that
map parameters to the whole field. A small residual weight helps a little and costs no
simulations; a larger basis beyond the first few modes and a different nonlinearity at
matched size do not. Before building any of it, compare with a coarse numerical solve:
on this column it is more accurate than the perceptron surrogates and cheaper below
about \nCoarseBreakEven\ queries (Section~\ref{sec:cost}). The reference itself
reproduces a published MOF-303 bed's two-wave shape and half-breakthrough time with
nothing fitted and misses its first-wave timing. Every withdrawn claim and caught defect
(\nLedgerA\ and \nLedgerB) is listed in Supplementary S1, generated from the ledger file
at every build.

# Corrections and Retractions

Every claim made in this project that was later withdrawn, why it was wrong, and what
replaced it. Started 2026-08-20, before any result was published — the point is that the
record exists from the beginning rather than being reconstructed later.

Two categories, kept separate on purpose:

**Part A** — claims that were asserted (in code, docstrings, planning documents, or
figures) and are now withdrawn.
**Part B** — defects caught before they contaminated any reported result. They corrupted
nothing; listing them as retractions would misrepresent the record in the other direction.

Corrections that run *in our favour* are recorded on the same terms as the others.

**2026-09-06 — the manuscript build.** **B64** is the build gate failing to see a
break in the document it guards: six macro references lost their backslash, became
ordinary words, and passed both existing checks. Caught by reading the rendered
prose rather than trusting the gate's own verdict.

**2026-09-06 — the figures.** **B63** came from building the dataset figure, which
needed the Damköhler distribution and found two of them under one name, plus a
justification statistic that existed only in a code comment.

**2026-09-05 — the bibliography.** **B62** was committed by the session writing the
file that exists to prevent it, and caught in the same sitting. Recorded in full.

**2026-09-05 — the L4b-v2 chain, read in flight.** **B60** and **B61** come from reading
the completed Q3 cells while the sweep was still running, rather than waiting for the
analyzer. One pre-declared invalidation condition has fired; one rule-4 gap was found
in time to be closed by a labelled post-hoc sweep instead of a re-run.

**2026-09-05 — citation tranche 4b.** **B54–B59** come from the re-run of the pass
that died on a session limit — the seven classical adsorption references. It found a
live identifier risk on L7's control (**B54**), that our isotherm is not the form we
name it after (**B56**), and that a 1982 USDA bulletin had predicted defect B8
(**B59**). Four primaries could not be read at all and are cited through verified
corroboration, which the ledger now records as a distinct state (**B58**).

**2026-09-05 — citation tranche 4.** **B45–B53** come from five parallel
verification passes, each instructed to try to FALSIFY what the project believed.
Two references turned out not to contain the claim they were cited for (**B47**,
**B53**), one author was missing (**B52**), and three papers were being cited for a
consensus that does not exist (**B46**). A fifth pass died on an account session
limit; seven classical adsorption references remain unverified and uncitable.

**2026-09-05 — the figure pass.** **B43** and **B44** come from building the ladder
schematic, which reads each rung's effect and CI from a results file instead of from
prose. Three of L2-v2's four quoted comparisons had no file to read from. Drawing a
figure only from data is a defect detector, not just a style rule.

**2026-08-30 — audit.** A18–A21 and B24–B33 come from a systematic audit of the whole
repository against the literature. Two of them (**A19**, **A21**) touch results the
project had called load-bearing, and **A20** applies a correction `CONTINUE_HERE.md`
had itself instructed and that was never carried out. The audit's full evidence,
*including a list of what it could not check*, is in `AUDIT_2026-08-30.md`.

## Standing rules

1. No number enters the manuscript from a failing `validate.py` category.
2. Every figure regenerates from a checkpoint. A plotting script that labels a model
   curve must call `load_state_dict`; the harness enforces this.
3. Single-seed numbers may not appear in any table, including appendices.
4. Comparisons are against the *strongest* configuration of the competitor, not the
   first one run.
5. Errors made by the analysis are recorded on the same terms as errors in the code —
   see B5, B6, B7.
